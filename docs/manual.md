# How Quietkeep works

Everything the app can do, what each screen is for, and where to look when
something is not where you expected.

Three depths, and you are meant to stop at whichever one answers your question.
**In a minute** is the whole idea. **When you want to** is how to do a thing.
**Every screen** is the complete list, for when something is on your screen and
you do not know what it is. The reasoning behind any of it — the research, the
sources, the things that turned out not to be true — is in
[Why it works this way](/why.html).

---

## In a minute

Put anything down. Quietkeep holds it and brings it back on its own. You never
have to remember to look.

That is the whole product. Everything else is how it does it.

Five things worth knowing, and then you can stop reading:

- **Nothing you write down can go quiet.** Every single thing is on a surface,
  under a clock, on the Menu, or inside something that has a clock. There is no
  fourth option, and the app refuses to store anything that would have one.
- **There is no overdue.** A date that passed is not a failure, it is a thing
  that needs a new plan, and it says so in those words. Nothing counts your
  misses, nothing tracks a streak, and no screen goes red.
- **You are offered one thing at a time.** Not a list to get through — one
  thing, with a short line saying why it is that one, and a way to say *not
  this* as often as you like. Saying *not this* records nothing.
- **Filing is optional, always.** Nothing needs a place, a person, a project or
  a goal. The app works with none of them. They make it better at answering
  questions when you have them, and cost you nothing when you do not.
- **It is yours and it is here.** No account, no server, no telemetry. Nothing
  you write leaves the device unless you export it yourself.

## When you want to

### Put something down

Type it in the box at the top and press **Hold it**. That is the whole capture.
It does not ask you anything — no project, no date, no category. Something
written down is offered as work in the words you wrote it in, from the moment
it exists.

**More room** gives you a bigger box for something longer.

### Decide what a captured thing is

*Sort things out* takes them one at a time and offers eight plain choices: do it
now, next action, waiting for, someday, reference, trash, put it somewhere, or
not this one. Each choice says what it means underneath.

You never have to do this. Unsorted things are still offered — last, behind
anything with a real reason to be asking — so the pile costs you a few taps of
*not this*, never a wall.

### Get something done

**Next up** offers one thing and says why it is that one: a date arrived,
something it was waiting on finished, a place it lives came round, or simply
that you put it down and have not touched it.

- **Done** finishes it.
- **Not this** moves past it, as often as you like, recording nothing.
- **Start smaller** turns a thing you cannot begin into the first physical step.
- **This one is heavy** says it is costing more than it looks.
- **That is enough for now** ends the session without ending anything else.
- **Just one thing** strips the screen to the single offer and nothing else.

### Narrow what you are offered

Open **What's the situation?** — the button under the capture box. Three
questions, and each one narrows both what you are offered and what the list
shows:

- **Where you are.** Anything with no place turns up wherever you are, so this
  can never empty the screen. A place put on a project reaches everything
  inside it, so one answer covers a lot.
- **How long you have.** Anything you never estimated fits every answer. At the
  long end it stops narrowing and points at the Menu instead, because a free
  afternoon is rarely short of time — it is short of the thing you want to do.
- **Who is with you.** Anything with nobody named on it still shows.

They compose: answer two and you get both. Nothing is ever taken away — the
line under the button says what has been narrowed, and everything still comes
back.

If you answer one and almost nothing changes, that is worth knowing rather than
guessing at: the report described below counts how much of what you are holding
any of these three questions can actually reach.

**Recognise this one?** names what you have just set so it returns in one tap.
That is for the meeting you have every Tuesday, or the shape of a free weekend.

### Find something

The search box above the list opens what it finds and changes nothing. It keeps
no record of what you looked for.

### See everything you are holding

**What you are holding** is the complete list, and the line above it — *nothing
here has gone quiet* — is the standing proof that nothing was lost.

- **How it hangs together** shows the tree, on request. It is never the screen
  you land on.
- **Looking at** narrows the list to one branch without hiding anything from
  the offer, the proof line or search.

### Say a thing belongs somewhere

On a thing's own panel: give it a **place** where it can be done, a **person**
it involves, a **role** it is for, or put it under something. None of it is
required and you can undo any of it.

A place on a project reaches everything inside it. So if you want work things
to stop appearing at home, say it once on the project rather than on every
item.

### Say you are waiting on somebody, or that they are waiting on you

**With other people** shows both directions. It never says how long you have
kept anybody waiting — there is no field to hold that, deliberately.

### Do something because you want to

**The Menu** is the list where nothing is asking. No clocks, no pressure, no
dates. You put things there yourself and they wait for you.

### Come back after a while away

**Welcome back** says what came round while you were gone. If a lot of dates
passed you can move them all to the Menu in one go — nothing deleted, nothing
marked done, all of it retrievable.

### Get it out

*Your data* exports everything as one file, and imports one back. An import
always starts a fresh store and never merges. Nothing you write is ever lost to
an update.

### When something is wrong

The app can write out what it knows about itself. Open the ⓘ and find **If
something is wrong**, then **Show me the report** — or tap the version stamp at
the bottom of any screen, which goes to the same place.

**It contains nothing you wrote.** No titles, no names, no notes, no journal
text — counts and states only. You can read the whole thing before you decide to
send it, and nothing goes anywhere on its own. **Copy it** or **Save it as a
file** when you want to hand it on.

It opens with what is actually wrong, if anything is, in plain sentences —
work no copy holds yet, a version that has not finished installing, storage the
browser has not agreed to keep. Then the shape of the store, so somebody helping
can see the size and kind of what you are holding without seeing any of it.

One section is worth knowing about even when nothing is wrong. **What the
situation can narrow** says how much of what you are holding is reached by a
place, names somebody, or carries a time estimate — and how many projects and
other containers already carry a place. Those numbers explain something that
otherwise looks broken: if almost nothing is labelled, answering *where are you*
changes almost nothing, because anything unlabelled turns up wherever you are.
That is the design and not a fault, and the section says so. It also points at
where a little filing goes furthest, since a place on a container reaches
everything inside it.

If a problem really needs the actual data, the export under *Your data* is a
complete copy — send that instead, and only if you want to.

## Every screen

The complete list, so nothing you meet is a mystery. Most of these appear only
when there is a reason.

### The work surface

- **Next up** — the one thing offered, with why.
- **Sort things out** — captured things, one at a time, with eight plain choices.
- **Needs a new plan** — a date went by. Not a failure and not a backlog.
- **Working on** — a timer is running on something.
- **Coming up for air** — a check after a long stretch on one thing.
- **That was a session.** — the close of one, without a verdict.
- **Carrying** — the weights you said you were carrying.
- **With other people** — what they owe you, and what you said you would do.
- **Worth a look** — things that are structurally odd: nothing feeding a goal,
  a project with no next action, an area gone quiet.
- **Chosen for today** — what you picked for today, if you picked.
- **Upkeep** — things that come back on a rhythm rather than on a date.
- **Welcome back** — what happened while you were away.
- **What you are holding** — everything, with the tree and the lens.

### Behind a button

- **What's the situation?** — where you are, how long you have, who is with you.
- **What comes back, and when** — every held thing and the date it returns.
- **Where the attention is** — what each role is carrying, and where your time
  actually went.
- **What you're working toward** — your goals, areas and outcomes, empty ones
  included.
- **How it hangs together** — the tree, as an inspection, never a workspace.
- **The Menu** — the list where nothing is asking.
- **What's here, and what you can open** — every block on the page, as a route
  to it.
- **Something on your mind that isn't a task?** — for the thing that is bothering
  you and is not work.
- **How you are, and anything weighing on you** — capacity and weights.

### The panels

Opening any item gives you everything about it in one panel — its words, its
date, its place, its people, its role, where it sits, its first step, its notes.
It is titled with the thing itself rather than with a name of its own, which is
why it is not in the list below.

The named ones:
- **More** — the way to everything below.
- **How it works** · **Help** · **Your data** · **Things you can do** ·
  **Settings** — the five destinations behind *More*.
- **Quietkeep** — the ⓘ: what this is, what it is not, how to install it, what
  changed, and the licence.
- **If something is wrong** — inside the ⓘ: the report the app writes about
  itself, which contains counts and states and nothing you wrote.
- **Stopping for now** — ending a timer without ending the thing.

## What it will never do

Stated because a promise is only worth what it refuses.

- **No streaks, no scores, no completion percentage.** Nothing accumulates and
  nothing can be broken.
- **No red.** Nothing on any screen escalates in colour to make you act.
- **No counting what you did not do.** No misses, no late tally, no per-person
  record of how long you kept somebody waiting.
- **No guessing.** The app never infers a place, a person or a project from
  your behaviour. It asks, once, or it does without.
- **No setup.** There is no wizard, no template to choose, and no screen asking
  you to get organised before you can use it.
- **Nothing leaves the device** unless you export it yourself.

## Why it works this way

Every choice above came from research on memory, attention and motivation, and
each finding is tagged by how well established it actually is — including the
ones that are contested, and the ones that turned out not to replicate.

That is in [Planning for Humans](/why.html), with the sources named.
