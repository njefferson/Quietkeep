# The glance: one surface above the doors, and one reading of when a thing comes back

**Status:** Proposed for release 3.27.0, a capability · **Date:** 2026-11-03
**Builds on:** `docs/decisions/design-review-2026-11.md` (the section "A proposed shape"),
`docs/structural-assessment.md` (Phase 1), ADR-0108 (the hub), ADR-0124 (the days ahead).
**Changes the event vocabulary:** not at all. Every fact here is a projection of events that exist.

This record is the whole of what steps 9 and 10 build. It was written from the source as it
stands on staging at release 3.26.2, and every file named below was opened before it was named.
Where a standing refusal in the repository meets the proposal, the record says so in the last
section and follows the refusal.

## 1. What this record decides

- One function, in a new file `src/reading.ts`, says which clocks of a node name a day and what
  that day is. The days-ahead view, the calendar file and the glance all read it. Nothing else is
  moved onto it in this release.
- One new section on the landing, `#glance`, titled **The weeks ahead**, sits directly above the
  hub's doors. It shows the days from today, the stretches between two dates, who is being waited
  on, and what is still to sort. It is a projection, stores nothing and asks for nothing.
- No door is removed. No noun, event, kind or relation is added. A lane is a derived shape, stored
  nowhere.
- Release 3.27.0 carries it. The accessibility walk, the manual, the plain-mode list and the
  release note move in the same commit as the surface.

## 2. What binds this, read from the record

- Law 3, no past bucket. Nothing before today is drawn. A passed hard date is a present replan
  card on its own surface (ADR-0034) and is not painted onto today here.
- Law 4. The glance is live work laid out in time. It is not an altitude view and shows no horizon.
- Law 5. One decay primitive, untouched. The glance reads clocks and never computes pressure.
- Law 7. No score, no count of the person, no rank, no percentage, no streak. Order and words
  carry every fact. No color carries pressure (ADR-0010, ACCESSIBILITY.md B-01).
- Law 9. All projection. Nothing is written by the glance.
- ADR-0085 and ADR-0113. The pile of things not yet sorted is not given a number on the screen
  arrived at. The changelog entry 3.23.13 says the same. The last section applies this.
- ADR-0108. The hub keeps its doors. The glance is above the doors and is a part of the hub, not a
  door, not a stance, and not a stop in the Contents list.
- The plain mode (`src/plain.ts`). A survey of dated days is the altitude that mode comes down
  from, so the glance is hidden there, by the same reasoning as `#dated-open`.

## 3. The one reading

### Where it lives

`src/reading.ts` (new). It imports only `src/fold.ts` (for `isAppClock`, `NodeState`) and
`src/time.ts` (for `DayShape`, `localDayKey`, `calendarDaysBetween`, `isValidIso`). It imports
neither `src/ics.ts`, `src/held.ts` nor `src/dated.ts`, because `src/ics.ts` imports `src/held.ts`
and the new file must sit below all three files or the modules form a cycle.

It moves out of `src/ics.ts`, unchanged in behavior: the tie order (`KIND_PRIORITY`), the set of
kinds that may carry an alarm (`CALENDAR_KINDS`), the test of whether a clock names a day
(`readerSet`), the walk over every such clock (`everyReaderClock`), the single soonest alarm clock
(`soonestAt`, which carried two unused parameters that are dropped), and the clamp of a passed
day to today (`calendarDay`). `src/ics.ts` re-exports `CALENDAR_KINDS` and `calendarDay`, because
`test/ics.test.ts`, `test/seam-audit.test.ts` and other tests import both names from there and must
pass unchanged. `exportsToCalendar` and `calendarEntries` stay in `src/ics.ts`: these two are a
selection of which nodes, not a reading of which day, and rest on `src/held.ts` and
`src/requests.ts`.

### The rule, stated once

- R1. A clock names a day for its node when its kind is due, start, suspense or park, or when its
  kind is review and a person set it. A review clock the app wrote names nothing. Which review
  clocks the app wrote is answered by `isAppClock` and by nothing else; the intent record
  (`CLOCK_INTENT`) is not edited by this release. Every clock a capture or a routing step receives
  from the gate is an app-written review clock, so a capture names no day.
- R2. The day a clock names is the local day of its instant at midnight in the reader's zone,
  never earlier than today. Midnight, and not the reader's movable day boundary, because the
  calendar file and the diary both break the day at midnight and the days-ahead view promises the
  same mornings as the file. The boundary is an input of the function (a `DayShape`), the three
  callers pass `atMidnight(zone)`, and a test pins that a boundary of 3 moves the day at 03:00.
- R3. In the days-ahead view and the glance a node appears once for each clock that names a day,
  soonest first, ties in the order suspense, due, start, park, review. In the calendar file a node
  appears once, on the day of its soonest clock among due, start, suspense and park. A person's
  review clock names a day in the view and carries no alarm in the file. This is the existing
  behavior, said once: the file is narrower than the view on purpose (ADR-0007, entry 15).
- R4. A lane is a node with a clock of kind start and a clock of kind due, both naming a day under
  R1, where the start's instant is not after the due's. It is one shape of one node. A node whose
  start is after its due has no lane and appears as two ordinary entries.

### The function

Names are fixed here so the three callers and the tests agree.

- `export type PlacingClock = { at: string; kind: string }`
- `export const CALENDAR_KINDS: ReadonlySet<string>` (moved, same members: due, start, suspense, park)
- `export function placingClocks(n: NodeState): PlacingClock[]`: every clock R1 admits, soonest
  first, ties by the tie order. Invalid timestamps are skipped, never thrown on.
- `export function alarmClock(n: NodeState): PlacingClock or null`: the soonest clock of a kind in
  `CALENDAR_KINDS`, ties by the tie order. This is what the calendar file reads.
- `export function laneOf(n: NodeState)`: returns an object holding the `start` and `due`
  `PlacingClock` of R4, or null.
- `export function dayNamed(at: string, nowIso: string, day: DayShape): string`: R2, a
  `YYYY-MM-DD` key never earlier than today's.
- `export function dayOffset(dayKey: string, nowIso: string, day: DayShape): number`: whole
  calendar days from today, never negative.

### What the two existing callers become

- `src/ics.ts`: `calendarEntries` takes its clocks from `placingClocks` when the view is asking
  and from `alarmClock` when the file is asking; `calendarDay(at, nowIso, zone)` returns
  `dayNamed(at, nowIso, atMidnight(zone))`. The `includeSoft` flag keeps its meaning and its name.
- `src/dated.ts`: `datedDays` already takes every day from `calendarDay`; it computes the day
  offset through `dayOffset` instead of its own subtraction. Its output is byte for byte the same.

The existing tests of both, `test/dated.test.ts`, `test/ics.test.ts`, `test/seam-audit.test.ts`,
`test/membership.test.ts`, `test/read-perf.test.ts`, `test/big-sample.test.ts` and
`test/taskpaper.test.ts`, pass without a single edit. That is the proof step 9 has changed no
behavior, and a test that needs an edit to pass is a defect in the step, not in the test.

### What this does not do

- It does not reclassify any entry of `CLOCK_INTENT`. Release 3.26.1 measured that moving a
  sorting-route clock to the app's side fixes the held card and breaks a bare next action, and
  left the move for after one shared reading exists. This reading leaves the intent record as it
  is and reads it through `isAppClock`; moving a candidate is the structural assessment's own
  later step.
- It does not touch `soonestClock` or `soonestDemand` in `src/held.ts`, nor `arrivedClock` in
  `src/nextup.ts`, nor the clock readers in `src/ui/detail.ts` and `src/ui/work.ts`. Of the
  thirty-two derivations the assessment counts, three lay dates out and move here; twenty-nine stand.
- It adds no event and stores nothing. The structural gate the assessment proposes (no read of
  `.clocks` outside the reading) is not built here; it cannot hold while twenty-nine sites remain.

### Step 9 tests, in `test/reading.test.ts` (new)

Each test builds its state from events through `fold`, as `test/dated.test.ts` does.

- a node with only a due clock names that day
- a node with only a start clock names that day
- a node with both is a lane, and `placingClocks` returns both, due after start in order
- a start after its due is no lane and both clocks still place
- a suspense clock places, and wins a tie against a due clock at one instant
- an app-written review clock alone places nothing and `alarmClock` is null
- a review clock a person set places in `placingClocks` and is absent from `alarmClock`
- a park clock places and is eligible for the alarm
- a node with no clock places nothing
- a clock with an invalid timestamp is skipped and the others stand
- a passed clock names today, never an earlier day
- `dayNamed` with a boundary of 3 at 02:30 local names the previous calendar day and at 03:30 names
  the new one, and with midnight names the calendar day in both cases
- over a mixed store, every node the file carries is on the same day in the file and in the view
  (the existing membership property, restated against the reading)

## 4. The surface, element by element, in the order a reader meets it

Everything below sits in `public/index.html` as one section, placed immediately after the
`.capture-extras` row and immediately before `#hub`, inside `<main>`. The capture line and the
coverage line are above it in the frame and are not touched. The markup carries a heading, the
sentence under it, and empty containers; every other word is written by `src/ui/glance.ts`, so
the shell's word budget moves by the heading and one short sentence only.

```
<section id="glance" class="glance" data-hub-part data-not-a-stop aria-labelledby="glance-heading">
```

`data-hub-part` is the existing marker that makes `src/reach.ts` (`jobsOf`) answer the hub and
makes the no-stance hiding rule in `public/app.css` leave the element visible on the landing and
hide it inside every job. `data-not-a-stop` keeps it out of the Contents list (`stops()` in
`src/ui/contents.ts` skips it), as `#hub` does. It has no `data-stance-name`, so it is not a door
and `help-check` does not count it among the stance names. There is no `role="status"` and no
`aria-live`: the block repaints with every render and announces nothing, because a standing
projection that spoke on every repaint would be the noise `tools/live-check.mjs` and
`tools/announce-check.mjs` exist to refuse.

In text order:

1. `<h2 id="glance-heading" class="section" tabindex="-1">The weeks ahead</h2>`. The heading text
   is new to the app, so the manual names it (section 9).
2. `<p id="glance-words">`. One quiet sentence of state: `Dated things, day by day, from today.`
   When nothing is dated in the thirty days: `Nothing you hold carries a date in the next thirty
   days.` Neither sentence names a failure, a count of the reader or a thing not done.
3. **The week.** An ordered list `#glance-week`, one item for each of the seven days from today,
   always seven, in date order. Each item opens with the day's heading, written by the existing
   `datedDayWords`, so the days read as the days-ahead view reads (`Today — Tuesday,
   November 3`, `Tomorrow — Wednesday, November 4`). Under it, the dated titles of that day, or the
   sentence `Nothing dated.` Each title is a button that opens the thing's own sheet, as a title
   does in the days-ahead view. Beside a title, in quiet ink, the existing `datedKindWords` for the
   clock that put it there (`due`, `starts`, `comes back`, `answer owed`), and for a waiting-for
   `with <name>`, taken from `withWhom`. No day is colored.
4. **The rest of the thirty days.** A second ordered list `#glance-month`, continuing the same
   items for days eight to thirty from today, but only for days that carry a title; a run of empty
   days is not drawn. If none carry a title the list holds one item, `Nothing else is dated in the
   thirty days.` The same title rows and the same words as the week.
5. **Longer stretches.** An ordered list `#glance-spans`, present only when at least one lane
   falls in the thirty days. One item for each lane, soonest due first, at most four. Each item is
   a title button, then one line of words, then a drawn track. The words are the reading of the
   lane: `Starts <day>, due <day>.` where a start already passed reads `Started <day>, due <day>.`
   and the days are written by the existing `clockDayWords`. The track is a row of thirty equal
   columns, `aria-hidden`, with the lane's days filled from the first column it covers to the last
   within the thirty. A lane that begins before today fills from today's column. A lane that ends
   beyond the thirty fills to the last column and the words state the real due day. The track is
   the only place extent is drawn; the words carry the same fact for anyone who is not looking at
   the track. When there are more lanes than four, one quiet line says how many more and a button
   opens the days-ahead sheet, which holds everything.
6. **Waiting on others.** An `<h3>` and a block shown only when the existing `waitingOnAnyone`
   returns something. At most three rows, in that projection's own order. Each row is a button
   that opens the thing's sheet on the naming control (`{ reveal: 'person' }`, as the people
   surface does), with the title and, in quiet ink, `With <name>.` or `Nobody named yet.` The
   duration `waitingWords` produces is not shown: the glance says who, and the people surface keeps
   saying how long. Under the rows, one button carrying the existing `peopleWords(total)` sentence,
   with `These <n> first.` appended only when more exist than are shown. Pressing it enters the
   People job (`enter('people')` in `src/ui/hub.ts`). `#people` is hidden exactly when
   `waitingOnAnyone` is empty (`src/ui/app.ts`, the person-lens block), so the button is present
   exactly when the job it enters is live.
7. **Still to sort.** An `<h3>` and a block shown only when the not-sorted-yet group of
   `heldGroups` is non-empty. At most three titles, in that group's own order (newest first), each
   a button that opens the thing's sheet. If more exist than are shown, one line says `More are
   waiting. These <n> first.` where n is the number shown, three, and no figure for the pile is
   written anywhere in the block. One button, `Open Not sorted yet`, presses through to the group
   in the complete list: the same sequence the where-everything-is sheet runs today (enter the
   held job, open the fold, scroll to the group labelled by its own title), extracted from that
   sheet's click handler in `src/ui/app.ts` into one function both callers call.

Bounds, in one place so the code and the tests read one list:
- `GLANCE_DAYS` 30, `GLANCE_WEEK` 7, `DAY_CAP` 5 titles on one day, `SPAN_CAP` 4 lanes,
  `WAIT_CAP` 3 rows, `SORT_CAP` 3 titles.
- A day with more than five titles shows five and one line, `<k> more on this day.`, with a button
  to the days-ahead sheet. The count is of dated things on one future day, a fact about the day.
- Passed hard dates are not drawn. `calendarEntries` marks such entries with group `replan`, and the
  glance skips them: a thousand imported dates gone by would otherwise become a thousand titles on
  today, which is the aggregate the gauge's comments record removing because it turns a fact about
  a date into a fact about the reader. Passed dates stay on the hub's own door for dates gone by
  and on today in the days-ahead view, unchanged. Within one day the glance keeps the days-ahead view's
  order (title, then id).

Empty and sparse states, each calm and each true: no dates, nothing waited on and nothing unsorted
is the heading and the one sentence of item 2. A store with no work has no hub, and the section is
hidden with it (section 6), so a newcomer's first screen does not change.

## 5. The projection: `src/glance.ts` (new)

Pure, no I/O, `now` and `zone` as arguments like every projection here. It reads the selection
through `calendarEntries(state, nowIso, zone, true)` and the reading through `src/reading.ts`; it
owns no selection of its own, for the reason `src/dated.ts` and `src/ics.ts` give at length.

```
glanceView(state, nowIso, zone): GlanceView
GlanceView: { words, week: GlanceDay[7], month: GlanceDay[], spans: GlanceSpan[],
              spansMore: number, waiting: { total, shown: GlanceWait[] },
              unsorted: { shown: NodeState[], more: boolean }, anyDated: boolean }
GlanceDay:  { day, offset, items: DatedItem[], hidden: number }
GlanceSpan: { node, startOffset, dueOffset, from, to, runsPast, startedAlready }
```

`DatedItem` is the type `src/dated.ts` already exports. Day words, kind words and whom come from
`src/dated.ts` and `src/people.ts`; the glance restates none of those words. The module exports its caps
as constants and the unsorted block exports no total.

## 6. Layout and styles, `public/app.css`

Tokens only, and only these: `--ink`, `--ink-soft`, `--bg`, `--surface`, `--line`, `--radius`,
`--gap`, `--target`. No `--accent`, no `--warm`, no new custom property, and so no new
foreground and background pair: ink and ink-soft on the surface and on the page are pairs the
review and people blocks already use and the contrast registry already measures. The filled
portion of a track is `--ink-soft`, which is also the non-text contrast a filled bar needs against
the surface; the words beside it carry the same fact, so the bar is never the only channel.

- The block is a card like `.review` and `.people` (surface, one-pixel line, radius). Titles are
  buttons shaped like `.review-open`: full width, `min-height: var(--target)`, left-aligned, the
  title with `overflow-wrap: anywhere`. Every title and every count button is at least 44 pixels
  tall at any text size, because `--target` is `max(2.75em, 44px)`.
- Phone width: one column, 16 pixel gutters from the page, the track a CSS grid of thirty
  `1fr` columns at full card width with a fixed short height. Day headings and rows wrap; nothing
  sets a width that can exceed the viewport. At 320 pixels and 200 percent text the block must not
  scroll the page sideways (the walk asserts it).
- Wide arrangement, 900 pixels and up: on the landing, `#glance` takes the same measure the hub
  takes (`max-width: 46rem; margin-inline: auto`), in the same rule family as the existing
  `#runway[data-hub]:not([data-stance]) main > #hub` rule. The week and the rest of the thirty days
  stack as at phone width; the wide screen gains margin and nothing else, so no second layout is
  introduced. In the job view the glance is hidden at every width, as every other non-hub,
  non-job child of `<main>` is.
- Hiding rules: the landing already shows `[data-hub-part]` and hides it inside a job, so the
  glance needs no new show rule. One rule is added, `#runway:not([data-hub]) #glance { display:
  none; }`, because with no hub (an empty store) the landing rule that hides non-hub children is
  not in force and the section would otherwise show.
- No inline `style` attribute anywhere: the site's policy is `style-src 'self'`. The track's filled
  span is set through the element's CSSOM (`el.style.gridColumn = ...`), which the policy allows,
  and never through markup or `setAttribute('style', ...)`.
- Nothing animates. Nothing is sticky.

## 7. Behavior, `src/ui/glance.ts` (new) and its wiring

- `mountGlance(session, now, deps)` returns `{ refresh }`. `deps` carries `openNode(node, opts?)`
  (the detail sheet's `open`), `goPeople()`, `goUnsorted()` and `openDays()`. The module reads the
  state, calls `glanceView`, and writes the section's children with `replaceChildren`. It holds no
  state of its own and writes no event.
- In `src/ui/app.ts`: mounted in a try block after the detail sheet is mounted (a title opens it),
  beside the search mount, so a broken glance can never cost capture, as every surface here is
  contained. `refresh` joins the list inside `rerenderLists`, so every render and every
  `refreshAll` repaints it before `paintHub`, which runs last and must stay last.
- `goUnsorted` is the shared function of item 7 above. `goPeople` is `enter('people')`.
  `openDays` opens `sheet-dated`, whose own painter reads the store on open.
- `src/plain.ts`: `'#glance'` joins `PLAIN_CHROME_HIDDEN`, with a reason in the list's own style,
  directly after `'#hub'` or after `'#dated-open'`. `tools/plain.mjs` fails on any region in
  neither list, so the commit that creates the section without this edit is red.

## 9. The walk, the manual and the help

### Accessibility walk, `tools/a11y.mjs`

A new `REGISTRY` entry, `'the glance'`, listing every selector the block uses, next to `'the hub'`
(line 1457 region): `#glance-heading`, `#glance-words`, `.glance-day-name`, `.glance-title`,

`.glance-open`. The registry refuses a selector that matches nothing in any walked state, so the
walk's own store must contain, on the landing, at least: a thing dated today, a thing dated within

helpers and adds those clocks and nodes through the same routes a person uses, never by writing
state directly. The landing is audited right after `'the hub'` in both themes, with the same five
audits that state runs: contrast, axe, accessible names, separation and targets, focus rings on
`.glance-title`, `.glance-count` and `.glance-open`. Each assertion below is a `pass`/`fail` line in
the walk's own style:

- text order: the section's headings and list items, read in DOM order, are the order of
  item 1 to 7; and the same elements sorted by rendered top edge are in that order too, so the
  layout cannot reorder what a screen reader reads
- targets: every `.glance-title`, `.glance-count` and `.glance-open` is at least 44 pixels in both
  dimensions' minimum, measured the way the walk measures every other control
- both themes, at 390 by 844
- enlarged text: at 320 pixels wide and 200 percent, the glance causes no horizontal overflow
  (≤1 pixel, the walk's own tolerance) and every title still wraps inside the card
- wide: at a viewport of 1024 pixels the glance is displayed, its width is at most the hub's, and it
  sits above the hub in the rendered order; inside a job at 1024 it is not displayed
- inside a job at 390 pixels, and with no hub (an empty store), it is not displayed
- presses: a title opens its own sheet and the sheet closes back to the landing; the waiting count
  button lands in the People job; the still-to-sort button lands in the held job with the group's
  heading in view, entered from the landing the way a finger enters it (not from inside the job,

- no pile count: the still-to-sort block's words contain no digit other than the number shown
- plain mode: with the one-thing module on, `#glance` is not displayed

## 10. Tests, `test/glance.test.ts` (new)

Built from events through `fold`, in the style of `test/dated.test.ts`.

- nothing before today is drawn: a store with a clock yesterday and a clock tomorrow draws only
  tomorrow, and the passed one is on no day of the week or the month
- a passed hard date (a group `replan` entry) is absent from every day and the days-ahead view
  still carries it unchanged
- a lane spans its two dates: a node with start in three days and due in ten has its span from

## 13. Cautions found while reading, each with what the build does about it

- The proposal says the not-yet-sorted group is shown with its count stated. ADR-0085 took the
  total off the arrival screen, ADR-0113 says in its own words that nothing it licenses reaches the
  arrival screen, and release 3.23.13 wrote that a count of the pile is the thing that screen has
  always refused. The landing is the arrival screen. The record follows the refusals: three titles,
  a statement that more wait, a press to the group, and no figure for the pile. The waiting-for
  block is counted, because the hub's People door already prints that same sentence. The plan's
  general rule that every bound states its count is met for the dated days, the lanes and the
  waiting block and is not met for the pile, deliberately. This is recorded as a finding for the
  next plan to answer, and a later release may overturn it only on evidence that a standing count is
  read as material and not as a debt, which is ADR-0113's own condition.
- The days-ahead view puts a node on a day for every clock it carries, so a lane node is on its
  start day and its due day and now also under *Longer stretches*. That repetition is accepted: the
  day rows state the date, the stretch states the length, and both read from one reading.
- Held groups break the day at the reader's movable boundary and the calendar, the view and the
  glance break it at midnight. The disagreement already exists between the held list and the
  days-ahead view; the glance joins the view's side and does not widen it.
- Within a day the glance does not reorder what the view orders (title, then id). The calendar
  selection arrives in held-group order and the view sorts it, so the glance sorts it the same way.
- `#glance` is a `<section>` with an id, so `tools/surfaces.mjs`, `tools/manual-coverage.mjs`,
  `tools/plain.mjs` and `tools/narrows-check.mjs` all see it. The first three are answered above.
  `data-hub-part` appears in the markup today only on buttons; if `narrows-check` objects to it on a
  section the step hands back FAILED naming the check, and does not weaken the check.
- The release raises the landing's rendered height. `tools/size-check.mjs` measures destinations at
  phone width and the shell's word count, and its header allows a deliberate, dated raise. That is
  the one gate whose own text names a path to a higher number, and it is the only one edited here.
