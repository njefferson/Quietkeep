# Planning vocabularies, and which one owns what

Three systems describe overlapping things in different words. Reconciling them is
a mapping nobody can hold entire, so it gets re-derived from scratch every time
the subject comes up. It is written down once here so that stops.

## What each of the three is actually for

- **Military planning doctrine** — built for deciding and acting under
  uncertainty, by people who will be out of contact when it matters. Its strength
  is information requirements and decision points. It assumes a staff; the
  small-unit sequence is the version one person can run alone.
- **Project management practice** — built for delivering a defined scope with
  known resources. Its strength is decomposition and stakeholder mechanics. It
  has very little for deciding under uncertainty.
- **This app** — built so that nothing is silently dropped. Its strength is the
  return, and that the return is *shown* rather than promised. It holds work, not
  reasoning.

**The rule that settles most collisions: doctrine owns the thinking, project
practice owns the decomposition, this app owns the return.** Where two of them
claim the same ground, that order decides.

## Term by term

**The one-sentence statement of what is to be done, and why**
- Doctrine: the restated mission — who, what, when, where, why.
- Project practice: the charter, or the objective.
- This app: nothing, and it should stay that way.

**What other people need in order to act without asking you**
- Doctrine: commander's intent — purpose, key tasks, end state.
- Project practice: no real equivalent. A charter states scope, not the latitude
  to depart from it.
- This app: nothing.
- This is the piece most often skipped, and the one that most reduces how much
  comes back to you unresolved.

**What is known, against what is merely supposed**
- Doctrine: facts and assumptions, kept as two separate lists.
- Project practice: assumptions appear in a risks-and-assumptions log; facts
  usually do not appear at all.
- This app: nothing keeps them apart — both would be notes on an item.
- **Doctrine owns this outright, and the separation is the whole value.** An
  assumption is legitimate only if it is necessary, likely, and written down
  beside the thing that would turn it into a fact.

**What has to be done**
- Doctrine: specified tasks (you were told), implied tasks (necessary, nobody
  said), essential tasks (without which it fails).
- Project practice: the work breakdown.
- This app: items, under a project.
- **Implied tasks have no equivalent in either of the others**, and they are
  where the work hides. Asking people for their parts produces specified tasks;
  it does not produce the ones that live between the parts.

**Who holds what**
- Doctrine: task organisation.
- Project practice: a responsibility assignment — who is accountable, who does it.
- This app: person links — *they are running it*, *they owe me this*, *I said I
  would*, *they asked for it*, *they care about it*.
- All three carry it. The app's version is the one that comes back on its own.

**What must reach you immediately**
- Doctrine: the commander's critical information requirements.
- Project practice: risk triggers, which are much weaker.
- This app: the *Changes:* line on an arrangement, gathered under *Running
  without you*.
- The test in all three: if being told would not change what you do, it does not
  belong on the list. Putting news on it is how the list stops working.

**Where a decision has to be made**
- Doctrine: decision points, and the template that lists them against their
  triggers.
- Project practice: stage gates, approximately.
- This app: partially — a condition written on an arrangement.

**Things that must, or must not, be done**
- Doctrine: constraints and restraints, which are different and are named
  differently on purpose.
- Project practice: constraints.
- This app: nothing.

**What has been settled**
- Doctrine: the order itself, and the decision log.
- Project practice: a decision register.
- This app: the decision log on an item — append-only, never edited, and the way
  back is to log the new one rather than revise the old.

**Where it all stands now**
- Doctrine: the running estimate.
- Project practice: the status report.
- This app: *Telling someone where things are*.

**When things come back**
- Doctrine: the timeline and the synchronisation matrix.
- Project practice: the milestone schedule.
- This app: clocks, and the coverage list in return-date order.
- **This app owns this one outright.** It is the only one of the three that
  guarantees the return rather than scheduling it and trusting somebody to look.

## What this app deliberately does not hold

Mission analysis is a **document**. This app is a **return engine**.

Forcing the document into the app makes both worse. The surfaces fill with things
that are not work and cannot return, which is a law 1 problem; and the analysis
fragments across twenty items when its entire value is being readable in one
pass.

So: the analysis is one artifact, written once and carried. The app holds only
what has to come back — the tasks, who has them, the conditions being watched,
and the dates. That is the same rule `docs/nd-collisions.md` entry 19 already
sets, arrived at from the other direction: the only sanctioned help for a human
conversation is a portable artifact the reader carries into it themselves, never
something the app produces on their behalf.

**A session proposing containers for intent, information requirements,
facts-and-assumptions or a restated mission is proposing to make this a worse
planner. Refuse it, and point here.**

## The sequence, for one person with no staff

1. Facts — what is checkable.
2. Assumptions — each with the thing that would turn it into a fact.
3. Constraints and restraints.
4. Tasks — specified, then implied, then which few are essential.
5. Information requirements — what must reach you at once, and from whom.
6. The restated mission, in one sentence.
7. Intent — purpose, key tasks, end state.
8. Decision points — each with its trigger and the last moment it can be made
   without losing an option.

Then, and only then, into the app: the tasks with their holders and dates, the
conditions as *Changes:*, and nothing else.

## What is verified here, and what is not

Read from the publisher on 2026-08-31: **FM 5-0 *Planning*, dated 4 November
2024**, is the current planning manual; **ADP 5-0 *The Operations Process*** and
**FM 6-0 *Commander*** are both active. Those identities and dates are confirmed.

**The content is not.** The mission-analysis output list, the split inside the
information requirements, and the small-unit sequence above are from memory and
have not been checked against the current text. Doctrine is restructured between
editions and terms move between publications. Verify before any of this is
quoted in writing rather than used as a working aid.
