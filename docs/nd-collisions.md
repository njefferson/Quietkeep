**The evidence marks, and what each licenses:**

- **Strong** — replicated experimental work, or meta-analytic. Safe to build a
  mechanism on.
- **Moderate** — consistent findings from a smaller or younger literature. Build,
  but keep the mechanism cheap to reverse.
- **Contested** — genuinely disputed. Refuse, or build only what is right for
  other reasons as well.
- **Community** — described consistently by the people it happens to, and not
  measured. Enough to REFUSE on, and enough to build a filter over facts the app
  already holds. Never enough to build an inference on.
- **Disproven** — claimed and not supported. It goes to the refusals in NOTES.md
  with its reason, so the idea cannot come back as a fresh thought.

**And the frame the whole catalogue sits in.** This app is a PROSTHESIS, not a
treatment: the 2025 Lancet Psychiatry component network meta-analysis found
non-pharmacological interventions lack demonstrated efficacy on core adult ADHD
symptoms, and what survives is external scaffolding at the point of performance.
No entry below may be read as a therapeutic claim, and nothing here is ever
framed as skill-building or as reducing the need for the app — that redefines
continued use as failure, which is a shame surface with no red pixels in it.

**Routing marks:** **V2-candidate** (buildable against the V2 plan), **later**
(real, not yet), **refuse** (best served by NOT building — the refusals are
load-bearing). Ranking proposals stay behind Q-11/Q-12 per NOTES.md's own rule.

Every routing proposal is written against the invariants: no "overdue", no
streaks, no scores, no red walls, no shame surfaces, no prescriptive "should",
rest is legitimate, one decay primitive, demand-free types, additive-only data
model.

### 1. Task initiation cost — knowing exactly what to do and being unable to begin

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — The gap between intention and action is the core ADHD deficit Barkley locates "at the point of performance": the knowledge is intact, the launch mechanism is not. Activation cost is highest when the task is large, vague, or aversive, and the cost is paid before any progress exists to reward it. The person sits in front of the task, fully aware, unable to start — which observers misread as unwillingness.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Strong.** The intention–action gap at the point of performance is among the best-replicated findings in the adult ADHD executive-function literature. What is weaker is any claim about *which* intervention closes it — see the prosthesis note in the header.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — A long list raises the activation threshold of every item on it, and priority matrices demand a comparison at exactly the moment comparison is impossible.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — The single computed Next-up card ("one thing, chosen for you"); the offer capped at two unalike options so choosing is preference, not comparison (ADR-0060); the do-now timer as a cheap entry price showing presence, never progress or countdown (ADR-0059); "Not this" cycles freely and records nothing. The azimuth check honestly names initiation as one of the thin halves.
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **SHIPPED — 1.28.0.** The first-step verb is on the offer itself (`#nextup-bite`), writing an ordinary child node under the offered item, exactly as proposed. The offer then holds the bite.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **SHIPPED** in 1.28.0, corrected from *V2-candidate* on 2026-08-17 — its own SINCE WRITTEN said built while the mark said candidate. A "smaller bite" verb on the offer card itself: name a first physical action without leaving the surface, writing an ordinary `node.created` parented under the offered item (covered by clause (d)), and the offer then holds the bite. Same pattern as 1.19.0's `filed` route — put the act where the stuckness is, instead of a trip through the detail sheet.

### 2. The wall of awful — emotional debt accumulated on an avoided task

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Brendan Mahan's wall of awful: every avoidance, failure, and disappointment attached to a task adds a brick, until what stands between the person and a ten-minute chore is a wall of feeling that must be climbed before the chore even starts. The task's difficulty is no longer the task; it is the history. This is why "it'll only take ten minutes" persuades nobody.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Community.** A coined model (Mahan) that describes something people recognise instantly and that has no controlled measurement behind it. It earns its place here as a description of what the interface must not do, not as a mechanism to build on.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Overdue-red and visible reschedule counts render the wall in the interface — "postponed 14 times" is the brick count read aloud — and each app-open adds mortar.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — The load-bearing refusals: no "overdue" anywhere because shame produces the avoidance that made the thing late (ADR-0010); "Not this" and "Not now" are event-free forever, so the app structurally cannot recite your avoidance back to you (ADR-0056); a passed date becomes a replan card — a present decision, never a failure state (law 3); the amnesty never implies there was something to forgive (ADR-0043).
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **SHIPPED as WEIGHT rather than as a pebble — 1.34.0.** "This one is heavy" is now said on the item in three words, and a low day reaches for lighter work instead of shortening the list. The pebble route was not taken: load is about the DAY and weight is about the THING, and conflating them would have made a fact about the work into a fact about the person. The refusal of avoidance *detection* stands unchanged.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse**, reversed from *V2-candidate* on 2026-08-17 after reading it against this entry's own paragraphs. The proposal was: let the detail sheet raise a pebble whose `affects` names the item, and let the app plot the co-occurrence. **That plot is the wall, rendered.** This entry's refusal is *"any avoidance detection — an inferred wall is the ledger this app exists to not keep"*, and a surface showing *the thing you keep not doing, beside what you said was weighing on you* is that ledger whether the app computed the link or the person drew it. The entry was arguing with itself: its SINCE WRITTEN already recorded the pebble route as not taken, with the reason, while its routing mark still said candidate. **`affects` stays in the vocabulary and stays unwritten** — the field is in `pebble.raised`, the fold copies it, `raisePebbleEvents` takes it with a default of `[]`, and no surface passes one. That is deliberate now rather than merely true. The half worth having shipped as WEIGHT in 1.34.0: said by the person, about the THING, with no day attached.

### 3. Out of sight, out of mind — a thing that leaves the visual field leaves existence

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Colloquially "no object permanence"; mechanically it is cue-dependent prospective memory failure (Einstein & McDaniel): intentions fire on external cues, and an item with no cue never fires. Filed means gone. The person keeps everything on the desk, the counter, and forty open tabs, because visible is the only kind of remembered.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Strong.** Cue-dependent prospective memory failure is experimental work with decades behind it (Einstein & McDaniel), and Gilbert's offloading studies make the cost of raising cue-setting effort measurable. This is the best-evidenced entry in the catalogue and it is the thesis.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Filing and archiving are the *virtues* of conventional systems — inbox zero is literally the instruction to put everything where it can no longer cue you.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — This collision is the thesis. Law 1's return engine: every node is on a surface, under a clock, on the Menu, or parented to something under a clock, and the write gate refuses anything else. The coverage gauge shows the proof ("everything returns · 0 silent"). 1.19.0's `filed` route makes the place, and the place carries the clock so its contents come back with it (ADR-0073).
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **SHIPPED in part — V2 stage 1.** The offer now says WHERE a thing sits (`#nextup-place`), which is the half of this that did not need the owner's use to settle. **And the bounded contents-on-return card shipped in 1.27.0** — `contentsWords` in `src/held.ts`, rendered on a return card in the held list's `ready` group and nowhere else, whose own docblock names this entry and this entry's top-ranked proposal. This bullet said that half was still open for fourteen days after it landed.
- **AND THE PROOF MOVED TO WHERE IT CAN BE READ — 2.8.1 (ADR-0099).** This entry names the coverage gauge as the mechanism, and the gauge was the first thing INSIDE the held list — 2.73 screens down on a phone, measured. So the one line that answers *is it still there* was only ever read by somebody who had already scrolled to the thing it was reassuring them about, which is a cue nobody uncertain about the app was reaching. It is now directly under capture. The same release put three entries behind Contents, and this collision is exactly why one of them — the load entry — reports its own state on the row that reaches it: a surface that goes quiet the moment it is out of sight commits the failure this entry describes.
- **AND THE OFFER NAMES CONTENTS TOO — 2.13.0**, which is a different surface from 1.27.0's and not a second delivery of the same thing: that one is the RETURN CARD in the held list, this one is the OFFER, in the tier that fires when a horizon has come round and nothing else is asking. When a horizon comes round, the offer now names what else is inside it: up to three titles, no dates and no acts, with the walk capped as well as the output so a large area cannot deliver its pile. The offered thing's own ancestors are skipped, because the place line already names them. Two defects were found by RENDERING it and neither by the tests — a stale bundle showing the project the card already named, and a first wording, *also in there*, that was ambiguous about which of the two containers was meant. `npm run look` renders the state and prints the line, because no walk can reach it: this tier is computed only when nothing else is asking, every walk fixture is a populated store, and the state needs a review clock already past on a container, which no date control in the app can set.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **SHIPPED in 1.27.0** (`contentsWords`). When a place's review comes round, its return card carries a bounded view of contents (capped per law 8) — which is that projection, on that card, with that cap.

### 4. Time blindness — the future is not real until it is now

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Barkley's temporal myopia: time exists as "now" and "not now", and things in "not now" carry no motivational weight until they cross the horizon and become an emergency. Duration estimation is also impaired in both directions. The person is not careless about deadlines; the deadline genuinely does not exist for them yet.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Strong for the phenomenon, moderate for the model.** Duration misestimation in both directions is replicated. Barkley's temporal-myopia framing is a well-argued account of it rather than a separately tested finding, and this document previously stated it as though it were the latter.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — A due date as the sole representation of time is a cliff: everything is silent until it is suddenly red, which is exactly the now/not-now boundary reproduced in software.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — The decay primitive makes time continuous — pressure rises as a gradient with no cliff to print (ADR-0010). Dependency dates compute latest-start and buffer burn from lead estimates, and a miss auto-replans (0.12.0). `.ics` export with `VALARM` puts the alarming in the OS calendar, which fires with the app shut (T1). Duration estimates are logged from v1 by deliberate decision so the data exists before the learning does.
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **SHIPPED — the approach vocabulary exists** (`approachOf` in `src/nextup.ts`), stating the computed fact in words on the card. Time itself was widened in V2 stage 5: the day now ends when the person says it does, and duration reads as a RANGE from their own history rather than a mean.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **SHIPPED**, corrected from *later* on 2026-08-17: this entry's own SINCE WRITTEN already recorded the approach vocabulary as built while its mark still said later. Verified in code — `approachOf` (`src/nextup.ts`) is computed on every offered item and rendered at `#nextup-approach`, over `dependencies.ts`'s latest-start and buffer arithmetic. Speak approach the way decay is already spoken: where an offered item feeds a suspense, the card states the computed fact in words. Facts, not urgency theatre; the fold already knew every number.

### 5. Interest-based motivation — importance does not start the engine; INCUP does

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — William Dodson's interest-based nervous system: activation follows Interest, Novelty, Challenge, Urgency, and Passion, not importance or consequence. A task can be acknowledged as critical and still produce nothing, while a fascinating trivial one produces hours. This is a different ignition system, not a values defect.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Community.** INCUP is a clinical coinage (Dodson), widely recognised by the people it describes and without controlled support as a taxonomy. Treat it as vocabulary, never as a rank.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — The Eisenhower quadrant assumes importance activates — its entire top row is a dead letter for this nervous system — and "just prioritize" moralizes the mismatch.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — The heat pass is a two-tap interest read (`heat.set`, ADR-0029); the Menu gives wants a home that owes nothing (law 6); a wish rides in every offer and structurally cannot become work (ADR-0060). But the azimuth check found that nothing about interest reaches `nextUp` — everything ranks on *when*.
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **SHIPPED — 2.7.0 ([ADR-0097](adr/0097-the-offer-reads-interest.md)), exactly as proposed.** Heat now breaks the tie inside the `ready` tier: hot, then unsaid, then cold, then creation order. The Q-11 gate was discharged by MEASUREMENT rather than by asking — every tier of `nextUp` was found to be temporal and the only tie-break inside one to be pressure then creation order (ADR-0095), which is the fact the guess was needed for. Q-12 is untouched: this reads a signal given deliberately in a flow that asks, and still learns nothing from declines. The entry's own binding is honoured three ways — it is a two-state fact the reader stated and not a computed score, it breaks a tie inside one tier rather than reordering any, and **the card says it out loud**, because an interest read that silently reorders the offer IS the hidden rank this entry forbids.
- **AND WHAT THIS ENTRY REFUSED, which mattered more.** The alternative on the table was ranking on IMPORTANCE — making "serves a goal" outrank "serves nothing", using the lineage ADR-0095 had just built. This entry forbids it in terms: the Eisenhower top row is a dead letter for this nervous system, and importance does not activate. It was drafted, and it was going to be put to the owner as a policy question. It did not need a ruling; it needed this paragraph read.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **SHIPPED** in 2.7.0, and it read *later, explicitly gated on Q-11 and Q-12* until then. The mechanism named here is the one that shipped: heat informing which candidate fills the offer's `ready` slot. The rule that held it — *do not build past Q-11 on a guess* — was right, and it was only half a decision, because nobody went and got the fact that would end the guess.

### 6. Autistic inertia and task-switching cost — hard to start, hard to stop, monotropism underneath

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Murray, Lesser & Lawson's monotropism: attention pools deeply in few channels rather than spreading thinly across many, so entering and leaving a task are both expensive state changes (Buckle et al. documented inertia as difficulty starting *and* stopping, independent of willingness). A forced switch does not just cost minutes; it can end the day's capacity.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Moderate for monotropism, community for inertia.** Monotropism has a stated theory and growing empirical work behind it; autistic inertia is described consistently by autistic people and has little formal measurement. Both are strong enough to REFUSE against, which is what this entry does.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Time-blocked calendars and pomodoro rotations slice the day into forced transitions, charging the switching toll a dozen times before lunch.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — Focus is state-level; the resume card is written *at the interruption*, in the same transaction, because "you do not get to press a button on your way out of the room" (ADR-0039); the five-word cue is invited later, never required; the comms sweep appears only on the focus-exit ramp when its own decay has come round, consolidating the check habit instead of adding interruptions (ADR-0042).
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse** any scheduled-rotation or timeboxing feature: a planner that initiates switches is charging the toll the design exists to avoid. The exit side is well routed; the entry side is entry 1's proposal, not a second mechanism.
- **THE OPEN QUESTION THIS ENTRY NOW OWNS — 2.8.1 (ADR-0099).** Three entries moved off the runway and behind a door, which trades a SCROLL for a SWITCH. This entry says switches are expensive and that is not a hypothetical to wave at; entry 1 says a screen of options is a wall in front of a start. They pull opposite ways here and neither settles it from a chair. The measurement that would: whether reaching the worry box through Contents is cheaper or dearer than scrolling past it was, in use, over days. That is not a refusal and not a proposal — it is a named thing to find out, and the release that raised it is reversible on purpose.

### 7. Hyperfocus — the gift that eats the day

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Hyperfocus (reviewed by Ashinoff & Abu-Akel) is total absorption with collapsed time perception and suppressed interoception — meals, appointments, and the rest of the plan vanish. It is the same monotropic pull as entry 6 running in your favor, until the day is gone to one thing and the exit lands with guilt about everything unmoved.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Moderate.** Hyperfocus has been measured and has instruments, but the literature is young and the construct's boundaries are argued over.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Conventional tools either spam interruptions (breaking the one genuinely productive state) or stay silent and let the dentist appointment die; none distinguish "a fact you must not miss" from noise.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — The hard landscape leads `nextUp`, so real appointments outrank everything; `.ics`/`VALARM` export means the OS alarm fires even with the app shut (T1); the focus surface's elapsed clock is the one thing that runs on its own, ticks in minutes, and writes nothing (ADR-0039).
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **SHIPPED — 2.7.1, and the delay is worth recording.** The line and its projection (`nextFixedToday`/`nextFixedWords`) were built in V2 stage 5 and rendered on the **work surface only** — the one an absorbed person has already left. So the routing proposal read as outstanding while its mechanism sat finished on the wrong screen, which is hub LESSONS §95's shape exactly: a feature that exists where the reader is not. It now renders on the focus surface too, from the SAME projection rather than a copy, so the two can never disagree. The words are a NAME and never a countdown, as proposed.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **SHIPPED** in 2.7.1, and it read *V2-candidate* until then. One fact line on the focus surface: the next hard-landscape item today, in words — no countdown, because a countdown is a deadline and adds aversion (ADR-0059's own reasoning). The OS alarm remains the guarantee; the line is the ambient horizon the absorbed person can catch peripherally.

### 8. Demand avoidance — a demand, including your own, triggers refusal

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — PDA (Elizabeth Newson's coinage; the community reframe is "pervasive drive for autonomy"): a perceived demand produces visceral avoidance regardless of the task's appeal, and *self-imposed* demands count — writing "go for a walk" on a list can make the walk impossible. The threat is to autonomy, and the response is not chosen.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Contested.** Demand avoidance as a distinct profile is genuinely disputed in the literature — recognised clinically in some places, rejected as a separate entity in others. The routing here is a refusal, which is the safest thing to build on contested ground.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Every conventional planner is a demand generator: due-dated everything, nagging reminders, "you should", a daily plan that is a wall of obligations by 9am.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — This is the best-defended collision in the app. Demand-free kinds are enforced at the write gate — a clock on a Menu item or pebble is *rejected*, not hidden (ADR-0014); acting on a want is a deliberate promotion, never an obligation that accrued (law 6); "Not this"/"Not now" record nothing, forever; the app plots and never prescribes (law 7); rest mode puts the Menu forward.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse** further building. The remaining ask — even Next up is technically an ask — is already answered by the Menu, the wish, and rest mode; softening the one offer the app makes would dismantle its purpose. The protection here is structural, and additions would only be copy.

### 9. Waiting mode — the 3pm appointment that consumes the whole day

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Community-named "waiting mode": an upcoming fixed event makes the preceding hours unusable, because working memory must hold vigilance against missing it, and starting anything risks the hyperfocus of entry 7 swallowing the appointment. The hours exist on the clock and not in capacity.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Community.** Waiting mode is near-universally described and essentially unstudied. The routing proposal is a filter over facts the app already holds, which is what makes it safe to build on a community account.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Calendars show the block but say nothing about the gap, and task lists keep offering two-hour work at 1:40 — reinforcing that nothing is startable, so nothing is started.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — The honest half, and it ships: the header clock states how much of the local day is left and how many open things are dated today — a count, never a list, and only clocks a PERSON set. Duration estimates have been logged since v1 so a later feature would not start empty-handed, and the `.ics` alarm holds the vigilance from outside the app, which is the part that makes a gap usable at all.
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **THE HONEST HALF IS NOW THE ONLY HALF — 2.12.2 ([ADR-0103](adr/0103-the-card-states-no-moving-number.md)).** This entry names the surface as well as the mechanism: *the header clock* states how much of the local day is left. The offer card was stating the same arithmetic too, in different words, on by default and uncoordinated with the clock — so anyone who had switched the clock on was told twice at once. The card line is gone and the clock keeps it. Nothing here changed; the app caught up with the sentence.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse** the time-of-day model this proposal needs, and with it the proposal — decided by the evidence in this file, not by the owner. It proposed something unbuildable for as long as it has existed. The proposal read: prefer items whose estimate fits the gap, and say "About an hour before Dentist." **The app does not know when anything happens.** Every clock is day-granular — `clock.set` takes a datetime and every writer in the app builds it with `endOfLocalDay`, and there is no time input anywhere in the markup — so both halves of that sentence are fabricated numbers, which is the one thing ADR-0010 exists to refuse. There is no "later today" to compute a gap against and no "next" among today's dated things to name, because with a day-granular clock they are all equally today.
  - **ADR-0075 already recorded this, about itself**: a release planned the same countdown, could not build it, and wrote down that "a capability was designed before the data model it needed was checked". This entry is that mistake a second time, in a different file, surviving because a research document proposes and an architecture record decides — and nothing held the two to each other.
  - **And the evidence in this catalogue decides the dependency against itself.** A time of day on a clock means a time field on every date control — which raises the effort of setting a cue, on every dating act in the app. Entry 6 grades that cost **Strong** and calls itself "the best-evidenced entry in the catalogue and it is the thesis": Gilbert's offloading studies make the cost of raising cue-setting effort *measurable*, and at high load it abolishes the benefit of offloading entirely. Waiting mode, the only thing the time would serve, is graded **Community** — near-universally described and essentially unstudied. **Strong evidence against, community evidence for.** That is not a close question and it is not a matter of taste.
  - **It was already decided once, in the release most likely to have added one.** The person-set day boundary moved where the day's edge falls and `docs/event-vocabulary.md` records, in the same breath, that it "adds no time-of-day to any clock — clocks stay day-granular (ADR-0010)".
  - **So this was never the owner's to decide, and routing it for a decision was the defect** — the same one the status record already names: a question answered by the evidence, handed over as a preference. What remains true is only the cost note from ADR-0075/0076 (it would touch `clock.set`, every date control, the `.ics` export and the replan path), and cost is not the reason. The correct build here is nothing, and the honest surface already ships.

### 10. Spoon budgeting — capacity is finite, variable, and invisible to the plan

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Christine Miserandino's spoon theory: energy is a small, countable, day-variable budget, and tasks draw it down unevenly. Overextension borrows from tomorrow at punitive interest. A plan built for the good-day self meets the actual day's self and loses.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Community for the metaphor, moderate for pacing.** Spoon theory (Miserandino) is a lived-experience metaphor; the pacing evidence it is often used to carry is real but comes from post-exertional conditions, and that is a DIFFERENT population from the one activation evidence comes from — the conflict this app resolves by asking once and obeying.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Rigid daily plans assume constant capacity; streaks and velocity metrics turn a low-capacity day into a visible failure rather than a fact about the body.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — `capacity.declared` is believed on its own — the app has no standing to ask for evidence about how you are (law 7, ADR-0065); pebbles record load with magnitude, three small things or one boulder narrow the offer — never below one, and the wish stays; the note reads "Fewer things, while you have this much on", where *while* is load-bearing: co-occurrence, never causation.
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **THE PREMISE WAS WRONG, checked 2026-08-17.** *"Down a sheet"* was never true: `#capacity-level` sits inside `<details id="load-entry">`, on the work surface, above every section and directly under capture. So the open question was never placement. What IS true is that the disclosure's summary reads *"Something weighing on you?"* — which names raising a pebble, a different act — and the capacity declaration lives behind it unannounced. **The reach defect is the LABEL, not the location**, and that is a smaller and more specific thing than the entry claimed.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **SHIPPED** in 2.7.2, and re-aimed by the check above before it was: the work was to make the one control's door say that BOTH acts live behind it, not to move a control that was already in the right place. The summary now reads *"How you are, and anything weighing on you"* — four words became eight, which the word budget had room for. It stays a collapsed door rather than becoming a standing question: an app that asks every morning how you are is a demand, and this is the best-defended part of the product against exactly that (entry 8).

### 11. All-or-nothing restart failure — one missed day reads as ruin

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Burns' all-or-nothing distortion compounded by Marlatt's abstinence violation effect: a single break is experienced as the destruction of the whole endeavor, so the rational-feeling move is abandonment. Restarting means facing the accumulated pile, which is the highest-activation task imaginable, so the lapse becomes permanent.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Community, with a moderate analogue.** The lived account is unmeasured; the abstinence-violation effect in the relapse literature is the nearest measured thing and is not the same claim.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Streaks are this collision built as a feature — ADR-0010 says it exactly: a streak's real function is the moment it breaks — and the re-entry screen of a conventional app is a wall of red.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — Re-entry is the *primary designed path* (law 8, ADR-0043): the greeting is bounded by its return type — counts only, no arrays, so no future surface can render the backlog; a weekend is not a lapse; the amnesty resolves every passed date to the Menu, marking nothing done and deleting nothing; Composed Today expires at midnight with no residue, and "what did I choose yesterday and not do" is structurally uncomputable (ADR-0051); a never-done item reads "ready again", never the loudest band (1.18.3).
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse.** This is the app's most completely routed collision. Anything added here — even a gentle "welcome back" flourish — risks becoming the record of absence the type system was built to make unwritable.

### 12. Novelty decay — every system works until it becomes familiar

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Dodson's N: novelty itself supplies activation, so a new planner runs beautifully for three weeks on the dopamine of newness, then the newness is metabolized and the system joins the graveyard. Guilt about the abandoned system then blocks return to *any* system.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Moderate.** The retention numbers are solid — median 30-day retention in this category sits near 3–4%. Attributing the drop-off to novelty decay specifically is an inference, and this entry previously read as though the mechanism were established.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Conventional systems depend on a daily tending ritual, so the moment novelty stops funding the ritual, the model collapses — and the return visit is greeted by the pile.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — The thesis makes resurfacing *structural, not habitual* — the app cannot depend on the user remembering to review, because that is the capacity it compensates for; levels push down so no review ritual is load-bearing (ADR-0013); the `.ics` half keeps working from the OS calendar while the app sits unopened; and when interest returns, re-entry is the designed path, not a fire exit.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse** manufactured novelty (themes, seasonal refreshes, gamified variety) — renting engagement from the mechanism that ends it. The honest residual risk is real, and the dogfood gate is already the instrument that measures whether the app survives familiarity.

### 13. Urgency addiction — only a closing door produces motion

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Dodson's U, formalized in Steel's temporal motivation theory: motivation scales steeply with deadline proximity, so everything happens in the panic window and the person learns to manufacture crises to function. It works, at compounding cost to health and quality, and it is the only lever that has ever reliably worked.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Community.** Widely described, and the closest measured relative is arousal-and-performance work that does not say what this entry says. Kept because the REFUSAL it justifies — never fabricate urgency — is right on other grounds.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Fake urgency everywhere — everything red, everything "due today" — teaches the nervous system to ignore the signal entirely, destroying the one working lever.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — It refuses to counterfeit urgency (no red walls, no cliff), and it computes *real* urgency honestly: latest-start from lead estimates, buffer burn, auto-replan on miss (0.12.0). The gradient never lies, so a rising signal stays credible.
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **SHIPPED with entry 4**, and corrected here on 2026-08-17: this was folded into entry 4's approach vocabulary, that vocabulary is built (`approachOf`, `#nextup-approach`), and so this mark was stale by exactly as long. The refusal half was never at risk — nothing in the app fabricates urgency, and 2.7.0 re-tested that by refusing to let an interest read cross a tier ahead of a real date.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **SHIPPED**, folded into entry 4's approach vocabulary: real deadlines surfaced early and factually give an urgency-calibrated nervous system a *true* closing door at a survivable distance. The refusal to fabricate urgency is the mechanism; keeping the true signal legible is the work.

### 14. Transition costs between contexts — "just quickly" shreds the day

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Task-switch costs (Monsell) are amplified several-fold in ADHD and autism: each context change pays a re-entry toll on the abandoned context, so five "quick" interruptions do not cost five minutes — they cost five re-immersions. The day ends with motion everywhere and progress nowhere.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Strong.** Task-switching costs are basic, replicated cognitive psychology, independent of any ADHD or autism claim.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Notification-driven tools initiate switches by design, and context tags label the contexts without ever paying down the return cost.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — The interruption *is* a capture (`interrupt.captured`) and the way back is written in the same transaction (ADR-0039); resume cards rank second, behind only a hard date; the comms sweep exists precisely because "each check is cheap, all of them together are the whole day" (ADR-0042); the lens changes what you look at, never what is held (ADR-0054).
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse** anything push-shaped beyond T0/T1. The routing here is subtractive: the app's job is to make interruption cheap to record and return cheap to find, both built — not to become another source of switches.

### 15. Rejection sensitivity meeting reminders — a nudge that lands as reproach

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Dodson's rejection sensitive dysphoria: perceived criticism or disappointment produces disproportionate pain, and a machine's neutral reminder is read with the same raw nerve — "You still haven't…" from an app is a disappointed voice. The adaptive response is to stop opening the app, and then the system is dead.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Disproven as stated; strong underneath.** RSD is not a distinct diagnostic entity and has no measure of its own — it is now a named refusal (NOTES.md). Emotional dysregulation in adult ADHD is well evidenced, and it carries everything this entry needs. The routing is unchanged; the warrant is corrected.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Overdue counts, red badges, and guilt-flavored nudge copy make every app-open an indictment; the badge number is a running tally of your failures on your home screen.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — Voice is enforced, not intended: the banned-vocabulary CI gate; "ready again" and pressure gradients in place of accusation; the greeting states a fact and never apologises on your behalf — a test asserts the absence of eleven specific formulations (ADR-0043); "Stopped. It is waiting for you", never "abandoned"; the sweep says "Last pass through your messages was 6 days ago", never "you haven't checked since Tuesday" (ADR-0042).
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **SHIPPED — the voice gate covers notification copy** (`tools/notify-voice.mjs`, in the battery). It landed in the V2 stage-7 run and this entry still read as a candidate until 2026-08-17, which is the stale-record defect this repo has now found in four places in one day. The constraint is in force BEFORE T2 exists, which was the whole point of asking for it early.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **SHIPPED**, and it read *V2-candidate, as a constraint rather than a feature* until then. T2 push is on the v2 Could list, and a push notification is this collision's delivery mechanism: the voice gate covers notification copy so no sentence leaves the app unmeasured. Cheap then, impossible to retrofit after the first bad push.

### 16. Choice overload — twenty options produce zero actions

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Iyengar & Lepper's classic finding, with Scheibehenne's meta-analytic caveat the thesis already records: the paralysis effect is real specifically where options are similar and stakes ambiguous — which describes every task list ever written. For someone already paying entry 1's activation cost, the comparison step is a second wall in front of the first.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Contested, and this is the correction that matters most here.** The classic choice-overload demonstration replicates poorly and meta-analysis puts the average effect near zero. ADR-0060's cap is still right, but it stands on the ACTIVATION cost of comparison at the moment of stuckness — a different and better-evidenced claim — rather than on choice overload as a general law. The decision does not change; the reason in the record does.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — The list *is* the interface: forty comparable rows, each a small demand, presented as the precondition to doing anything.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — Triage shows one card and asks one question, forced choice (ADR-0029); the offer is capped at two, at most one per reason, unalike by construction so choosing is preference rather than weighing (ADR-0060); even the timer's length options live in Extras, not at the point of starting, because "showing options to someone stuck at activation is choice overload where it costs most" (ADR-0059).
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse.** ADR-0060's own overturn clause holds the line: a third and fourth offer class is a comparison again. Routed; the work is to keep it routed.

### 17. Working-memory loss between capture and action — "why did I write this"

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Working memory (Baddeley) holds the context that made a captured fragment meaningful, and it drops that context within hours. The capture succeeds; the retrieval cue rots. At triage, "call about the thing" is a stranger's note, so the item is routed blind, or trashed, or — worse — kept out of vague guilt.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Strong.** Working-memory limits and the loss of encoding context are foundational and uncontroversial.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Inbox-zero pressure demands fast processing of exactly these stale fragments, and the interface shows the bare text as if the words were ever the whole message.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — Capture is zero-chrome and demands nothing at write time, which is correct — the moment of capture cannot afford questions. Interrupt captures pair automatically with the focus they interrupted (ADR-0039), which is context by construction; the note field and the resume card's five-word cue exist for deliberate context. An ordinary dump capture, though, arrives at triage as bare words.
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **SHIPPED — 1.29.0.** The triage card states capture context as assembled fact (`src/capture-context.ts`), pure projection over the log, co-occurrence only.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **SHIPPED** in 1.29.0, corrected from *V2-candidate* on 2026-08-17 — its own SINCE WRITTEN said built while the mark said candidate. The triage card states capture context as assembled fact — when it was written, and what was in focus if anything was. Pure projection over the log; every datum already exists; zero new events; co-occurrence only (law 7). The same "context assembled" move law 3 already makes for replan cards, applied one surface earlier.

### 18. The planning/doing gap — planning as the most respectable procrastination

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Sirois & Pychyl's finding that procrastination is short-term mood repair explains why system-tending is its favorite disguise: reorganizing the list *feels* like progress, repairs the mood, and defers the aversive task — while looking, to yourself and everyone else, like diligence. Meta-work expands to fill the anxiety available.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Community.** Recognised everywhere, measured nowhere in this form. The refusal it justifies is cheap and reversible, which is the right posture for evidence of this strength.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Conventional systems reward tending: grooming, tagging, re-prioritizing, and perfecting the setup are always available, always virtuous-feeling, and infinitely deep.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — Law 4 is the structural answer: the runway is the only workspace, altitude views are inspection modes, the user never climbs (ADR-0013); Review is exceptions-first — a top handful of computed problems, never a tree to garden; goals and areas are cheap, optional, and demand no maintenance; there is very little surface *to* polish.
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **THE CONSTRAINT WAS HONOURED when the work it constrained shipped — 2.5.0.** Law 4's downward projection now exists ([ADR-0095](adr/0095-what-a-thing-is-for.md)) and it arrives as exactly what this entry demanded: a computed line saying what a thing serves, explicitly *never a destination*, with no door to a horizon and nothing to rearrange. The entry did its job by being read before the build rather than after.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse** curation surfaces, permanently — and carry this entry as a design constraint on the law-4 projection work: when higher horizons inform the runway, they must arrive as computed signal, never as a new place to rearrange things.

### 19. Body doubling and external accountability — another person's presence unlocks the start

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Body doubling (from the ADHD coaching community, often credited to coach Linda Anderson): the quiet presence of another person dramatically lowers initiation cost — not through supervision but through co-regulation and a gentle anchor to the present task. External accountability similarly externalizes the launch mechanism that entry 1 shows is impaired.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Community.** Body doubling has enormous lived support and very little controlled work. The routing refuses to simulate it, which needs no evidence at all.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Productivity apps counterfeit it as surveillance — shared streaks, public commitments, shame-as-a-service — which converts a co-regulation need into a rejection-sensitivity trap (entry 15).
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — Nothing, and mostly rightly: no accounts, no social features, no telemetry are named refusals on the Won't list. The timer's pulsing presence mark is the app's one, honest "something is here with you" gesture (ADR-0059).
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse** simulated or networked accountability. An offline single-user app cannot ethically fake a person, and attendance-keeping is the forbidden ledger. What it can honestly do already exists: the printable today-card is a sharable artifact a reader can hand to a real human — a partner, a colleague, a body-doubling call — on their own terms, with the app never knowing.

### 20. Special-interest integration — interests as fuel, not guilt

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — For autistic and AuDHD people, special interests are the deepest and most renewable motivational reservoir (the monotropism literature treats them as attention's home state, not a distraction from it). Systems that classify interest time as "unproductive" convert the person's best energy source into a guilt source, and the guilt then contaminates the interest itself.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Moderate.** Interest-driven engagement in autistic people is studied; the planning-tool application here is an extrapolation from it.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Eisenhower files interests under not-urgent/not-important; "reward yourself after the real work" frames the interest as dessert that must be earned — an obligation gate on the one thing that never needed one.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — The Menu is a first-class, structurally safe home for wants — a clock on one is rejected at the gate, so wanting never becomes owing (ADR-0014); a wish rides in every offer as a door, not a demand, and on a heavy day "the thing you actually wanted is the most appropriate offer in the set, not the least" (ADR-0065); rest mode puts the Menu forward.
- **SINCE WRITTEN** — **BOTH GATES DISCHARGED 2026-08-17, and the answer is a REFUSAL rather than a build.** Q-11 fell to entry 5's interest-aware offering ([ADR-0097](adr/0097-the-offer-reads-interest.md), 2.7.0). Q-12 was then answered from this document: learning what pulls somebody from what they DECLINE is avoidance data, which entry 2 refuses as *"an inferred wall"* and entry 8 refuses as further building on the best-defended collision in the app. **A signal somebody chose to give is not the same object as one inferred from what they avoided**, and the app now has the first — so it can be interest-aware without ever keeping the second.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse** anything algorithmic here, reversed from *later* now that both its gates are answered. The routing is the existing guarantee, worth stating as such in the app's own words: time on the Menu is never measured, compared, or earned.

### 21. Follow-through on other people's pieces — delegated things evaporate

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Prospective memory (Einstein & McDaniel) fails hardest for intentions with no natural cue, and "waiting for Sam's reply" has none: no artifact on the desk, no scheduled moment. The choice degrades to obsessive checking or total evaporation, and the evaporation is discovered three weeks later, in front of the person it inconvenienced.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Community.** A description of what happens, not a finding.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Most tools model only your own actions; a waiting-for is either a fake task that clutters the list or a note that never resurfaces.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — The waiting-for route is one tap in triage and lands with a three-day return clock (ADR-0029); the person lens attributes it when a name exists and shows the unattributed honestly, because one-tap capture makes unattributed the commonest kind (ADR-0040); request slots and the Not Now ledger keep decisions about other people's asks (ADR-0056); suspenses and the delta report carry the tracked-portfolio half (ADR-0041, ADR-0068).
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse** new mechanism; the machinery is unusually complete. The azimuth check's "waiting-for: zero after real use" reads as a places problem (nothing could be filed anywhere pre-1.19.0), not a missing feature — let the `filed` route bed in before concluding otherwise.

### 22. Rest guilt — the inability to rest without the rest being spoiled

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — After a lifetime of "lazy" as the explanation for executive dysfunction, rest arrives pre-poisoned: the person is too depleted to work and too guilty to recover, achieving neither. Burnout research in autistic adults (Raymaker et al.) names sustained masking and overextension without recovery as the mechanism; rest is not a luxury input here, it is maintenance.
- **EVIDENCE** — **Community.** Widely described, unmeasured, and the routing is a refusal, so the evidence bar for acting on it is low by construction.
- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Streak-keeping and activity graphs make a rest day visibly emptier than every other day — the calendar heat-map's pale square is a small public accusation.
- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — "Rest is legitimate" is law 8, not copy: **(correction, 2026-08-30 — REST MODE DOES NOT EXIST.** `NOTES.md` struck it as shipped in 0.18.0 and that release delivered the re-entry greeting and the amnesty only; a repo-wide search finds it in prose and in no source file. Every one of those refusals survives on machinery that IS built — demand-free kinds at the write gate, the wish carried in every offer, ADR-0065 — so none of them collapses, but the citation was wrong in three places and is corrected here rather than left to be discovered a fourth time. The nearest shipped thing is `Just one thing`, `src/plain.ts`, which caps the offer at one and keeps the Menu reachable, and which its own header insists is not a capacity signal.**); a weekend is not a lapse; absence is greeted with "You were away a fortnight. Everything you put down is still here" — a fact, not a bill (ADR-0043); nothing anywhere renders a completion count over time, so a rest week cannot produce a lower number on any surface.
- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse.** Any activity summary, however kind its intent, creates the comparison that spoils the rest. The epigraph is the routing: it holds the rest, so you can rest.

### 23. Cold start — the situational app arrives with nothing situated, and it was built never to ask

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — A person meets this kind of app one of two ways: carrying a few hundred to a few thousand lines out of wherever they were keeping things before, or writing down one thing, because writing it down was never the part that was hard. Either way, the app they have just opened knows how to do several things for them — show only what fits where they are, say how a role is carrying, name what a stray action is quietly serving — and none of it can do anything yet, because every one of those depends on a word that only exists once somebody has said it: a place, a person, a container, a goal. Nobody has been asked. So the first honest screen is not a curated one. It is everything, ranked on nothing but a clock, because a clock is the only fact the app currently has about any of it — not because the person has not decided what matters, but because the app was never told, and correctly never demanded to be told before it would even open. This is not a hypothetical: it happened, once, exactly, against a real 1,173-item import, and the reading of it afterward is in this repo's own history — eleven of fourteen node kinds sitting at zero, and every situational feature this catalogue has been routing toward for twenty-two other entries switched off at once, on the one day it mattered most that they were on.

- **EVIDENCE** — **Community, and specifically in the sense the header above defines it: felt consistently, measured hardly at all.** The pieces underneath are a different story and should not be allowed to lend the combination a strength none of them individually earns for it. That upfront multi-field configuration costs real, measurable effort is about as **Strong** as psychology gets: working memory is famously small (Miller, 1956; refined to roughly four chunks by Cowan, 2001) and cognitive load theory (Sweller, 1988) is exactly the account of why a block of N questions costs more, cognitively, than the same N questions asked one at a time on different days with nothing else competing for the same working memory. That a non-optional request produces resistance, and that visibly preserving choice measurably reduces it, is also close to **Strong** — psychological reactance (Brehm, 1966) is among the most-replicated findings in social psychology, and "directive framing produces more resistance than choice-preserving framing" is closer to settled than to hypothesis. That the population-specific mechanism is demand avoidance is **Contested** — this is entry 8's grade, not a new one, and it is not re-argued here: PDA's status as a distinct profile is genuinely disputed (O'Nions, Christie, Gould, Viding & Happé, 2014, built the first trait measure, the EDA-Q; a 2026 systematic review in the *Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders* found the evidence base still cannot settle whether it is a distinct entity or, as its own title asks, "a case of circular logic"). Declarative-language practice (associated with speech-language clinician Linda K. Murphy) — asking indirectly rather than instructing, widely taught inside PDA/autism practitioner circles — is **Community**: consistently described by practitioners and the people they work with, and this search found no controlled trial of it. One adjacent finding earns its own mention for the import half specifically: temporal landmarks measurably increase aspirational behaviour and let people file a shortfall under a "previous self" rather than the current one (the fresh-start effect — Dai, Milkman & Riis, 2014, a large multi-dataset field study, close to **Strong** on its own terms) — general population, not this one, and about motivation following a landmark rather than about being asked a question, but real support for treating an import as an arrival rather than a debt, which is the shape ADR-0043's amnesty already uses for a *returning* reader. And the actual comparison this entry exists to weigh — one declinable question asked in the flow, at the moment it would change the answer, against the identical question asked in a block at the door — **has not been tested, for this population or any other.** The nearest real research tests timing in adjacent domains and finds "later, in context" winning whenever it has been tried: just-in-time adaptive interventions in mobile health (Nahum-Shani, Smith, Spring et al., 2018, *Annals of Behavioral Medicine*) are built on exactly this logic for health behaviour generally; context-aware medication reminders outperform fixed-schedule ones in the adherence-informatics literature; and mobile operating systems moved from install-time to runtime permission prompts because install-time consent was neither read nor understood (Felt et al. and the runtime-permissions literature that followed), with comprehension measurably better at runtime even though it stays incomplete there too. None of that is this claim. It is consistent with this claim, at a comfortable distance, in other domains, on other populations, for other outcomes. This entry states the distance on purpose rather than letting proximity read as proof.

- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — The productivity-tool industry treats a taxonomy as a prerequisite rather than an outcome. The dominant methodology of the last twenty-five years is explicit that its method does not work until a trusted system exists, and its adopters describe the founding pass as a multi-hour undertaking before the system pays for itself; this repo's own stress-test import came from a planner built on exactly that premise, which is *why* it arrived pre-loaded with a taxonomy nobody here had built. Notion, Asana and Todoist gate the first capture behind a template chooser or a "set up your workspace" flow — a comparison-shaped decision (entry 16) placed at the one moment there is the least evidence available to make it, before a single real item exists to judge a template against. Several tools turn the setup itself into a scored checklist — a profile-strength meter, a percentage of "workspace configured" — recruiting entry 5's gamification refusal on top of everything else. And the source system behind this repo's own import demonstrates the failure at its worst: nothing filters and nothing surfaces until the tagging structure exists, so the tool meant to reduce cognitive work front-loads the heaviest cognitive work it will ever ask for, at the exact moment the person has the least investment in paying it.

- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — More than it gets credit for, and the one piece that matters most is still a plan rather than code. Contexts (ADR-0092, `src/contexts.ts`) and roles (ADR-0096) are both built structure-optional by construction: an unlabelled node fits every context, so filtering by place does nothing at all — never hides, never breaks — on a store where nothing has been named yet, which the ADR calls "load-bearing... so the feature cannot make the app look empty and broken on the day somebody first tries it." The horizon line that says what a thing serves (ADR-0095, `src/serves.ts`) makes the identical refusal one level up: it says nothing rather than offering a promote-button on an empty goal or area, and `docs/horizon-models.md` names the alternative and refuses it by name — "alignment theater... promote-buttons for empty altitude nouns" — citing this app's own eleven-empty-node-kinds finding as "the bill for the last time it was ignored." Capture itself asks nothing structural of anybody: a thing is offered as work in the words it was written in from the instant it exists (ADR-0086), with no container, context, role or goal required for it to be actionable. The import path already carries one hard-won cold-start lesson from before this entry existed: law 9 seeds a fresh store and never merges, and `src/taskpaper.ts` (around line 325) already refuses to convert a stranger's stale dates into fresh demands — a real OmniFocus export carried 1,173 due dates, the earliest from 2019, and every one is dropped on import rather than becoming, in the code comment's own words, "seven years of residue arriving as today's demands." The nearest existing precedent for the gesture this entry wants at arrival is the amnesty (ADR-0043), built for the opposite direction — a reader coming *back* after a lapse, not arriving for the first time — but the shape is exactly transferable: one stated fact, one bulk resolution, nothing marked done, nothing deleted, no interrogation of any item individually. What is genuinely not built is the mechanism this entry is about: ask once, in the flow, at the moment the answer would change something, declinable, never repeated. `docs/plan-situated.md` — dated today — names this almost verbatim as Phase 1's deliverable ("Situation is asked for at the moment it would change the answer, never up front... one question, in the flow, declinable, never asked twice") and its own resume-state block records Phase 1 as not started. And the failure this entry describes is not projected — it is measured: the azimuth check (2026-08-04) read a real 1,173-item import and found eleven of fourteen node kinds sitting at zero, with the first reading of those numbers wrong before a second reading found the actual cause.

- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **V2-candidate**, because this is less a new proposal than the evidence grade `docs/plan-situated.md` already said Phase 1 needed before it could start. Build one mechanism, not a first-run special case: the first time an offer, a filter or a ranking would differ depending on an unanswered situational fact — place first, since it is already wired (ADR-0092) — ask it once, in the flow, at that moment, worded as declinable as "Not this" already is, and never ask again about anything already answered. Extend the identical mechanism to role and to filing-a-container, riding the rule the `filed` route already settled (ADR-0073): a container gets made where work already is, never by promotion, never as a precondition. On import specifically: state the arrival as a fact, not a debt, in one line modelled on the amnesty's own words — how many things arrived, and that nothing is filed yet because filing was never asked for — and extend the `taskpaper.ts` precedent from clocks to ranking, so an import's sheer size does not out-rank an ordinary day's captures for no better reason than volume, which is exactly the azimuth check's second finding ("everything ranks on *when*... nothing ranks on what matters") asking to be closed from the other end. Keep the mechanism cheap to reverse, per the Community grade above: this is a filter over facts the app already computes, not a new inference engine, and it should stay that way. **Refuse**, explicitly and by name: any setup wizard, template chooser, or "let's get you organised" first-run flow — this is entry 8's and entry 18's refusal, restated at the door instead of mid-use. Refuse any completion meter or percentage for how much structure exists — "40% categorised" is a bar, and ADR-0096's role readout already refused a bar for a reason that applies here unchanged. Refuse inferring a context, role or container from behaviour rather than asking — that is entry 2's and entry 8's avoidance-ledger refusal, aimed at structure instead of at tasks, and the Community grade above says explicitly that this evidence is enough to refuse an inference on and never enough to build one. And refuse treating this entry as licence for the rest of `docs/plan-situated.md`'s later phases under cover of "fixing cold start" — this entry backs the ask-once-in-context mechanism and the import-arrival framing only; phases 2 through 8 already have their own plan and should clear their own evidence where they need it.

### 24. Situated retrieval — one offer answers what is next, never what fits where you are

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — A person does not only ask "what's next" in the abstract; they ask it bounded by a moment — where they are, how long they have, who is in front of them, how much is left in the day. A single computed offer that ignores all four still returns a correct answer to a question nobody asked. Put to a full day's real use rather than to its gates, this app returned exactly that, fourteen times in a row: the right task for the wrong moment, offered as though the moment did not exist. The person is then left to do the situational filtering by hand — hold the room, the clock and the list in their own head and cross-reference — which is the unaided working-memory operation this app's own thesis (entry 3) already says fails. An app whose entire premise is that a thing out of sight leaves existence should not require the reader to keep "what fits here" in their head to get a usable answer out of it.

- **EVIDENCE** — **Strong for the retrieval mechanisms actually in play; the product claim built on top of them has no grade in this set, because nothing has measured it.** Three literatures get reached for here and they do not carry equal weight. - Event-based prospective memory and implementation intentions (Einstein & McDaniel; Gollwitzer) are the ones that actually bear on *intention* retrieval, and they are already this app's own best-evidenced ground — entry 3's foundation, and the `situation` field's own citation. Both describe a specific, self-bound cue firing recognition in a person's own memory; neither describes a system computing a filter on that person's behalf, which is a different mechanism wearing the same word. - Godden & Baddeley's environmental context-dependent memory — the diving experiment usually reached for first, and the one worth being wary of — is real and replicated, but narrower than its reputation: Smith & Vela's 2001 meta-analysis finds the effect concentrated almost entirely in *free recall* of material with no other cues available, and it shrinks toward nothing once recognition is possible or other retrieval cues are present — the "outshining" pattern, where context-matching matters most exactly when nothing else is helping you. A person reading a titled item on a screen is doing recognition, not free recall from nothing, so this literature transfers weakly to what a filter interface actually does, and it is routinely overstated in productivity writing for exactly that reason. It also does not point one direction only: Radvansky's event-boundary work, already cited in this app's own canon, finds that *crossing* a context boundary degrades access to what was active before it — context effects are not a case for "more matching is always better." - On duration, the planning fallacy (Kahneman & Tversky; Buehler, Griffin & Ross on the corrective "outside view") is strong and well replicated, and this app's own entry 4 already grades ADHD duration misestimation as strong-for-the-phenomenon. But this evidence argues *against* a mechanism, not for one: it is a reason not to trust a self-reported number as a filter boundary, not a reason to build the filter on that number. - On list size, Iyengar & Lepper's original choice-overload demonstration is already correctly downgraded elsewhere in this catalogue (entry 16); the more useful citation here is Scheibehenne, Greifeneder & Todd's 2010 meta-analysis — the source of that null-average result — which also finds the effect concentrated where options are hard to compare and the chooser has no clear prior preference. That is a moderator finding, not just a null one, and it independently supports the comparison-cost reasoning ADR-0060 already adopted for its own reasons. - What is genuinely untested, in any of this: whether *specifying* a situation (one tap, one field) carries anything like the activation cost of *scanning* a list, long or short. Say that plainly, because it is the actual crux and nothing above answers it.

- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Conventional tools fail this in both directions. Flat task lists offer everything to everyone regardless of where they are or how long they have, which is entry 1 and entry 16's wall by another name. At the other extreme, auto-scheduling tools invert the failure: they silently compute a timeline from estimated durations and calendar gaps and present it with the authority of arithmetic, laundering a planning-fallacy-biased guess into a confident schedule and telling the person what to do with their day rather than showing them what is true about it — the exact thing product law 7 (the app plots, the human interprets) exists to refuse.

- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — More than the open questions suggest; the gap is wiring, not absence. - The place axis is **built and shipped** (2.2.0, ADR-0092). `context.attached` links a node to any number of person-named, demand-free context nodes (`src/contexts.ts`); "where you are" is a *device* kv preference (`WHERE_KEY`, `getWhereNow`/`setWhereNow`), never an event, because law 7 keeps a trail of where somebody was all week out of the log on purpose. `fitsHere` is a pure predicate where an unlabelled node fits *every* answer — the load-bearing default that keeps the filter from turning an unlabelled store into an empty screen. It is wired as a *post-filter*, not a re-rank: `src/ui/work.ts`'s `refresh()` (around 576–599) takes `workSurface`'s already-ranked head-plus-behind, filters both through `fitsHere`, and promotes the first surviving item to head, recomputing `total` as "what fits" — never a count of what is hidden. `nextUpQueue`'s ranking and its tests are untouched, exactly the move ADR-0060 made the last time the offer's shape changed. - A second mechanism already exists for the when-or-where-I-meant-to question specifically: the `situation` field (`situationOf`, `src/fold.ts` — "The SITUATION a person attached to this — when or where they mean to do it, in their own words"; written via `situationEvents`, `src/ui/detail-intents.ts`) is free text, self-authored, never validated into a shape, and it rides on every `NextUpItem` (`src/nextup.ts`) to be shown back verbatim wherever the ordinary ranking happens to surface that item. It is a working implementation-intention mechanism, citing Gollwitzer and Gawrilow & Gollwitzer in its own docblock. It is not a query surface — it cannot be asked "what mentions the kitchen," and it plays no part in ranking or filtering. - Duration data already exists, twice over, and neither is wired to anything that filters. `estimate.recorded` folds to `n.estimateMinutes` — the person's own guess, read by `estimateOf` (`src/duration.ts`), never scored against outcomes. `do-now.timed` folds to `n.timedMinutes`, a list read by `timedRange` as `{shortest, longest}` — deliberately never averaged, because task durations are "tau-heavy" and a mean sits where nothing actually lands. Both are display-only today. - The person axis has its data half-built too: `personView` (`src/people.ts`) already separates what a person is owed from what is shared with them; it has no surface. - And this entry is not the first document to name the gap: `docs/plan-situated.md` already records these same fourteen questions, already phases place (done), duration and "what's on my plate" (its Phase 3), the promise/person direction (Phase 4) and a unified situated surface (Phase 5) — and already commits, in Phase 5, to arriving at a filtered offer by *answering* a situation rather than *picking a screen* from a list of fourteen. This entry is the evidence gate that plan names itself as blocked on.

- **SINCE WRITTEN — ALL THREE V2-CANDIDATE AXES HAVE SHIPPED, and this entry read as three open proposals for weeks after they did.** Place: `fitsHere` and `WHERE_KEY` (`src/contexts.ts`). Duration: `fitsWithin` and `HOW_LONG_KEY` (`src/duration.ts`). Person presence: `fitsWith` (`src/people.ts`). All three are composed at one call site as post-filters over the unchanged ranking (`src/ui/work.ts`), which is exactly the guardrail this entry made the other proposals depend on. **The saved-situation shortcut shipped too** and stores only `{ context, minutes }` — no person — so a situation carrying who is in the room is not yet recallable. What the entry got right and is worth keeping: the crux it named — whether ASKING costs less than SCANNING — is still unmeasured, and entry 27 now supplies the adjacent finding that the cost of RECORDING the axis determines whether there is anything to filter on at all. That is why a filter over a store with almost no contexts reads as broken rather than empty.

- **THE QUESTION THIS ENTRY CANNOT CLOSE** — Whether asking costs less than scanning is not decidable from a chair. Treat it the way entry 6 treats its own open question rather than forcing it into a build/refuse binary: named, shipped small and reversible, and watched in real use for whether a declinable one-tap question ever becomes, for the person standing at the door unable to start, one more door.

- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **V2-candidate** across three axes, with one guardrail that the other three depend on and four refusals. - **V2-candidate** — Extend the shipped place pattern to duration, unchanged in shape: a device kv preference beside `where.now`; a pure predicate (`fitsWithin`) beside `fitsHere`, where *no estimate at all* — the ordinary case, since estimating is opt-in and undemanded — fits any window, exactly mirroring "unlabelled fits everywhere"; a post-filter in `work.ts` beside the existing one; `nextUpQueue` untouched. When history exists, filter on the *range's* wider end, never the bare guess, and say "estimated," never promise. Declinable, asked only at the point it would change the answer, never a gate in front of the offer's first paint. - **V2-candidate** — At the long end of that same control (a stated hour, a stated weekend) the right answer is not a longer duration-sorted worklist — that mistakes the constraint. A block of open time is rarely duration-limited; it is want-limited. Route a long-duration answer toward the Menu (law 6) and its heat signal (entry 5 / entry 20, already shipped), not toward a bigger version of the short-window filter. - **V2-candidate** — Extend the same pattern to `personView` for presence: of the three axes this is the best-evidenced, because a specific person standing in front of someone is the most distinctive, focal event-based cue of the three — closer to Einstein & McDaniel's strongest case than a generic room ever is. - **V2-candidate, cheaper than the above** — "What's on my plate, enough to decide whether to finish early" mostly asks for better surfacing of what already ships (the coverage gauge, law 2; the header clock, which ADR-0103 already made the one place that states how much of the day is left), not a new predicate. Route it there first and build new machinery only if that turns out not to answer it. - **later** — The recurring-meeting-materials question and the what's-between-me-and-this-person question both wait on the person axis landing and are not separately specified yet. Do not build a bespoke "meeting mode." - **refuse** — Any inference or auto-tagging from the free-text `situation` field into structured contexts or into ranking. The evidence for a situated cue rests on plans the person wrote *themselves*; a machine-mined version is a different, unevidenced object, and mining a person's own free text for signal is exactly what law 7 and the assist-consent rules already forbid elsewhere. - **refuse** — Any gap-fitting against clock time ("you have 23 minutes before your 3pm"). This is entry 9's refusal, unchanged and undisturbed by anything here: the schema has no time-of-day anywhere, and a duration filter answers "does this fit a length you name," never "here's what fits before your next thing." - **refuse** — A hard cutoff that treats `estimateMinutes` as fact for exclusion. The planning-fallacy evidence is strong and it argues for a soft sort or a label, never a gate that makes a mis-estimated item disappear from a window it was actually going to blow past. - **refuse** — A menu of named situational screens: one for the meeting, one for the weekend, one for the person. Entry 16 and Phase 5's own commitment agree — every axis stays a filter over the *one* offer, composed together, never a set of destinations to choose among. This is the guardrail the other proposals above depend on; building any one of them as a separate screen instead of a filter is the same mistake regardless of which axis it happens to.

### 25. Outstanding promises — what you owe someone evaporates the same way entry 21's incoming half does, and the discovery is theirs to make

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Entry 21 already names the mechanism for things delegated to you: an intention with no natural cue degrades to obsessive checking or total evaporation (Einstein & McDaniel). A promise made TO someone else is the same intention, aimed the other way, and it is not spared the mechanism — if anything it starts with an advantage the incoming direction lacks. The social-prospective-memory literature (Meacham, 1988; Brandimonte, Ferrante, Bianco & Villani, 2010, on an activity-based task with an explicit social motive; the review in Kliegel, Martin, McDaniel & Einstein, 2001, on importance manipulations) finds intentions framed as mattering to another person get executed more reliably than equivalent private ones, at least as single tasks under controlled conditions. So this is not a motivation problem. It fails for the identical reason entry 3 and entry 21 already document: no artifact, no scheduled moment, nothing left on the desk once the conversation where it was promised has ended. What is different from entry 21 is who discovers the failure and what it costs when they do. An unfulfilled waiting-for costs the reader a delay. An unfulfilled promise is discovered by the person it was owed to, and read by them as a fact about the reader — which is a different and heavier kind of failure to have surface three weeks late, in a corridor, the same way entry 21 describes.

- **EVIDENCE** — **Strong for the mechanism and its real-world cost; Disproven for the reason this catalogue used to reach for.** The prospective-memory half inherits entry 3's grade directly — same finding, same citation, no new claim. That the cost is real and not merely felt is also well evidenced, from both sides of the exchange: a 79-study systematic review covering 68,275 people across 22 countries (Hotte-Meunier et al., 2024) found employees with ADHD receive lower performance ratings not only from themselves but from coworkers and supervisors, worst on job-specific follow-through — that is OTHERS' judgment, independently recorded, not self-report. Fletcher's 2014 NBER analysis of the Add Health cohort found roughly a third lower earnings and 10–14 points lower employment among adults who had had ADHD, controlling what a large observational cohort can control — correlational and one country, but the direction is consistent with the smaller literature around it. On relationships, Eakin et al. (2004, Journal of Attention Disorders, N=33 ADHD-affected marriages against 26 controls) is the useful study precisely because it does NOT say the simple thing: the ADHD-affected partner self-reported worse marital adjustment, but the SPOUSES' own average adjustment scores did not differ significantly from control spouses — what did differ was that 96% of those spouses said the partner's symptoms interfered with their own functioning, and 92% said they had built compensations around it. That is others' testimony about the practical cost, distinct from the reader's own shame about it, and the two do not fully overlap. Divorce-rate figures from the Milwaukee longitudinal cohort (Barkley, Murphy & Fischer's synthesis) are cited everywhere in this literature at roughly double the base rate; that number reaches this document at one remove from the primary text, so treat it as directionally right rather than exact. **On why it feels like an indictment rather than a delay: the catalogue's existing refusal of RSD as a designed-around entity checks out, and checks out more solidly than what is currently cited for it.** William Dodson's coinage was never successfully peer-reviewed in its original form — by Dodson's own account the attempt ran roughly a decade and never succeeded, because the construct had no measure. A 2026 scoping review of the term's use even in the more hospitable autistic-adult research space (van Asselt, Reekers & Roke, 2026, screening 1,285 records down to 12 eligible studies) found conceptualizations and operationalizations vary considerably and that no study has evaluated an intervention for it — there is, at present, categorically nothing to design around, not just insufficient evidence for the thing this catalogue already declined to build. A separate 2026 peer-reviewed critical reflection (title on PubMed: "Rejection sensitivity dysphoria: a critical reflection") interrogates the term's clinical uptake directly. The clearest sign the premature reification has real cost: a published clinical commentary asks, in its own title, whether alpha-2 agonist medication is being prescribed "to treat Rejection Sensitivity Dysphoria in ADHD" ahead of any evidence the target exists in the form being treated. What IS real and well-measured: general rejection sensitivity as a construct (Downey & Feldman, 1996, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology — the Rejection Sensitivity Questionnaire, attachment-theory grounded, in use for three decades, applied to ADHD samples with real correlational findings, e.g. Müller, Mellor & Pikó, 2024) and emotional dysregulation as a probable core ADHD feature (Beheshti, Chavanon & Christiansen's 2020 meta-analysis, 13 studies, N=2,535, adults with ADHD scoring significantly higher than controls; a much larger literature makes the same finding in children, a different population, which this document keeps separate rather than borrowing its numbers). Entry 15 already answers this correctly for reminders; this entry inherits that answer rather than re-deriving it, because the underlying fact — heightened reactivity to perceived judgment, whatever its full cause — does not change direction when the reminder is about a person instead of a date. **What has no grade because nobody has run the experiment: whether SEEING a standing list of what you owe other people helps you keep more of it, or teaches you to stop opening the list.** The nearest real findings cut in different directions and neither answers the actual question. The most commonly cited "evidence" that visibility plus accountability helps — Matthews' 2007 study, "more than 70% of participants who sent weekly updates to a friend" succeeded against 35% of those who kept goals private — was never published in a peer-reviewed journal; it is a faculty conference presentation, self-selected (people who agree to send a friend updates are not a random sample), and is widely mis-cited online as a Harvard study it has no connection to. The one well-controlled EXPERIMENTAL finding on telling other people your intentions points the other way: Gollwitzer, Sheeran, Michalski & Seifert (2009, Psychological Science) found identity-related intentions NOTICED by another person were acted on LESS than ones nobody knew about, because being seen to intend produces a premature sense of already being the person who did it. That paradigm is a different mechanism from a private list only the reader reads — an AUDIENCE effect, not a CUEING effect — and does not transfer cleanly to what this entry proposes, but it is the one piece of experimental evidence in either direction, and it does not say what the popular "accountability helps" claim assumes it says. Between those two poles: no study has looked at a persistent, multi-item, private ledger of social obligations in an ADHD or autistic population at all.

- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Shared task tools model this as "assigned to me," aged by a due date, frequently colour-coded by how overdue it is, sometimes rolled into a per-person or per-project completion percentage — a visible reliability score attached to your name, readable by the person who assigned it. Inbox-zero culture treats an unanswered message as a personal failing to clear rather than a fact about attention. None of this is built for the person on the other end of the promise; it is built to make the promise legible to whoever is owed it, which is a different design goal from helping the person who made it remember to keep it.

- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — This is a deliberate asymmetry, not an oversight, and it should stay deliberate rather than become an accident of which side got built first. `RELATIONS` in `src/people.ts` names `requested-by` in the same closed set as `waiting-on`, but only `waiting-on` gets machinery: `isOpenWaiting`, `personView.owes`, `waitingOnAnyone`, `openDays` and `waitingWords` — a cross-person aggregate, a duration fact stated as a duration and never a verdict, sorted longest-open-first. `requested-by` gets one line: `src/ui/detail.ts` renders it as "Sam asked for this," a passive fact on the item's own sheet (`RELATION_WORDS['requested-by'] = 'they asked for it'`). In `personView` itself, a node carrying a `requested-by` link that is not an open waiting-for already lands in `involves` — but `involves` hard-codes `days: null` for every entry, so even on a person's own page there is no fact about how long it has been open, and there is no cross-person equivalent of `waitingOnAnyone` for this direction at all: nothing answers "who is currently waiting on me" the way `waitingOnAnyone` answers "who am I waiting on." The one thing that IS fully built for the outgoing side is the DECLINE: `src/requests.ts` and ADR-0056's Not Now ledger record a decision not to carry someone else's request, on purpose, as "the thing to point at when the same request comes back." That machinery is complete and this entry proposes nothing to it. The gap is upstream of any decline: an ordinary, still-open, not-yet-declined, not-yet-done task that happens to carry a `requested-by` link is otherwise indistinguishable from any other task, and nothing gathers those by person.

- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **V2-candidate**, built as a reversible bet and watched rather than assumed to work. Add `owedByAnyone(state, nowIso, zone)` to `src/people.ts`, mirroring `waitingOnAnyone` exactly: walk `heldNodes(state)`, keep nodes carrying a `requested-by` link that are not done (`!n.lastDone`) and carry no standing decline — reuse `standingDecline` from `src/requests.ts` rather than re-deriving the same fact a second place, the exact lesson ADR-0040 already states about `personName`. Add `owedWords(total)` beside `peopleWords`, same cadence: "Nothing is waiting on you right now." / "One thing is waiting on you." / "N things are waiting on you." Surface it in two places that already exist rather than a new screen: (1) the person lens, giving `requested-by` links their own labelled group instead of dissolving into `involves`, with a duration fact where one is honestly available — check whether a per-link timestamp survives the additive, non-LWW fold `people[]` uses before promising "since" words; if it does not, leave it null, the same restraint `openDays` already applies rather than inventing a date from a node's creation stamp, which could easily predate the promise itself; (2) the printable today-card in `src/today.ts`, directly beside the existing `waitingOnAnyone` section and built from the same projection, per that file's own stated principle that a second definition of "what matters today" would eventually disagree with the first. The today-card is the right home for the repair half of this: entry 19 already establishes the app's only sanctioned way to help a human conversation along is a portable artifact the reader carries into it themselves, never anything the app says on their behalf. A line that lets the reader be the one who raises it — "I still owe you that, sorry, this slipped" — before the other person has to, is the repair the MUM effect literature (Rosen & Tesser, replicated across decades) says people otherwise avoid initiating; the fix is not telling the reader faster, it is making it cheap to remember before they are asked. **Refuse, specifically:** any duration-based colour or urgency escalation on this list — a plain fact, exactly `waitingWords`'s register, never louder; any per-person tally shown anywhere except that person's own opened page — no roster with numbers beside names, which is the shame-by- arithmetic ADR-0042 already names and refuses; any "reliability" or "completion rate" metric, ever, in any form, including an implied one from showing counts over time — this is the single most tempting next feature and the one law 5 and ADR-0056's own "no per-person decline counts" clause most directly forbid; any UI distinction between a promise the reader volunteered and one they were asked for — the thin PDA evidence available says self-imposed demands are not exempt from the same refusal, the self-determination literature separately notes a self-authored commitment can still read as externally imposed in form, and the app has no honest way to classify the difference without a new question at capture time, which entry 17 already establishes as the wrong moment to ask anything; any in-app messaging, sharing, or notification to the person who is owed something — entry 19's refusal of networked or simulated accountability stands unchanged, this is memory support, never a channel; and no new `declined`-style event for this surface specifically — an item leaves it by being done, or by going through the existing Not Now route, which already covers that decision completely. Ship it small, watch whether people open it or start avoiding the person lens the way entry 15 already documents happening to notification-bearing apps in general, and take it back out if the second thing happens — it is one projection and two render sites, and removing it costs nothing else, the same reversibility entry 3 claims for its own line.

### 26. The reward-led menu — a real mechanism, a borrowed name, and a list that must never be owed

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — At the moment of being stuck, generating an activity that would actually help is itself a demand on the exact capacity that is missing — a second instance of entry 1's activation cost, stacked on top of whatever produced the stuckness in the first place. A list made in advance, at a calmer moment, and *consulted* rather than *composed* removes that one step: no decision about what might help, only a decision to do one of a handful of things already judged worth doing. Popularly this is a "dopamine menu" — activities sorted by size or cost, kept ready for the moment nothing else is working. The relief, when it lands, is real.

- **EVIDENCE** — **Community for the practice as packaged; Strong for one mechanism underneath it, borrowed across a population it was not built for.** The term itself is traceable only through secondary aggregation rather than any primary publication — every independent source converges on a 2020 YouTube video by Jessica McCabe (*How to ADHD*), organising activities as menu tiers (appetisers, entrées, sides, desserts, specials), with TikTok carrying it wide in 2023. No controlled evaluation of the packaged practice exists under that name or any other. What it borrows is real but arrives with its own terms attached. Behavioural activation and pleasant-event scheduling (MacPhillamy & Lewinsohn's Pleasant Events Schedule, 1971; meta-analyses since — Cuijpers et al. 2007, Mazzucchelli et al. 2009/2010 — place behavioural activation among the best-evidenced depression treatments there is, large effect sizes, replicated) is genuinely Strong, and it predates "dopamine" being attached to this idea by fifty years. But it is evidence for restoring positive reinforcement against depressive anhedonia — a different mechanism from an ADHD activation deficit — and applying it here is an extrapolation across a population boundary the literature itself does not cross, the same shape this catalogue already flags for spoon theory (entry 10). ADHD delay-discounting findings (Marx, Hacker, Yu, Cortese & Sonuga-Barke, 2021 meta-analysis, 37 comparisons, 3,763 participants) are genuinely Strong for choosing smaller-sooner over larger-later, but say nothing about whether a prepared list fixes it — the imaging literature finds the blunted step is reward *anticipation*, not a shortage of options. INCUP, often invoked beside this practice, is Community, exactly as entry 5 already grades it — no new information here. And "dopamine" is not doing the work the name implies: Berridge's well-replicated wanting/liking dissociation found dopamine tracks anticipatory wanting and reward-prediction error, not consummatory pleasure, and the ADHD-specific dopamine-transporter imaging literature is itself unsettled — Volkow et al.'s larger, better-controlled studies found *lower*, not higher, transporter availability in unmedicated adults, reversing smaller earlier studies, with enough overlap with controls to limit any individual reading. The name borrows scientific-sounding legitimacy a 1970s behavioural technique never claimed for itself.

- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — "Reward yourself after the real work" makes the want contingent on the obligation — entry 20 already names this exact pattern — and the one genuinely well-evidenced mechanism for contingent reward (Premack's principle, 1965: a high-probability behaviour reinforces a low-probability one *when made contingent on it*) is evidence for precisely the structure this catalogue refuses elsewhere. Self-care and wellness apps routinely turn "did you use it today" into a streak or a check-in, converting relief into another tracked performance. A single fixed list, reused without judgement, meets hedonic adaptation (Brickman & Campbell's "hedonic treadmill," 1971; Frederick & Loewenstein, 1999: repeated exposure to the same stimulus produces a literally weaker response) — the same handful of items quietly stop paying out, and nothing in the format notices or says so.

- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — More than nothing, and less than the practice actually calls for. The Menu (`aspiration` kind) already carries every structural protection this practice is reaching for, enforced at the write gate rather than left to judgement under stress: it cannot carry a clock at all — `src/gate.ts` rejects `clock.set`/`park.set` against one outright (ADR-0014) — so no contingent or scheduled version is possible even by accident. It is consulted, never composed under stress: `offerNow` in `src/offer.ts` rides exactly one Menu item into the Next-up offer beside real work (ADR-0060), "Not this" cycles it for free and records nothing — the same refusal against inferring anything from declines that entries 2, 8 and 20 already hold — and ADR-0065 explicitly protects the wish from the one place a heavy day narrows anything: work narrows, the wish does not, because "on a low stretch the thing you actually wanted is the most appropriate offer in the set, not the least." Nothing tracks whether an item was ever used: the complete set of Menu events is `menu.item.added`, `menu.item.removed`, `menu.item.promoted` and `save-for.updated` — there is no "consulted" and no "done," so the hedonic-adaptation risk named above cannot even be measured by the app, let alone shown back as a demand. What genuinely is not there: the six-category schema (`MENU_CATEGORIES` in `src/menu.ts` — Read/Try/Go/Make/Look into/Save for) is chosen by exactly ONE route in the shipped app: the bulk picker in the sort sheet (`#sort-bulk-category`, `src/ui/sort.ts`). Every single-item route decides for the reader — the detail sheet's "On the Menu" button calls `toMenuEvents` (`src/ui/detail-intents.ts`), which takes a category and is called from `src/ui/detail.ts` without one, so it falls to its `'read'` default; the someday/reference triage route (`src/ui/triage-intents.ts`) hardcodes `'read'`; replan writes `'try'`. So the Menu cannot be a menu of different KINDS of thing, because almost everything arriving one at a time is filed as one kind. So the categories that exist today are an activity-type taxonomy nobody actually populates, not a cost or effort tiering, and the offer's own wish-selection (`wishes()` in `src/offer.ts`) walks the Menu in creation-id order regardless of category. There is no way today to mark anything as small versus involved, and no way for the offer to reach for a small one when capacity is thin.

- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse** for the practice as packaged, with one narrow **V2-candidate** and one **later** named below so neither is reinvented from zero. - **refuse** any named "dopamine menu" feature, any use-tracking or "you haven't checked your menu" prompt, any contingent or earned-reward framing, any algorithmically suggested items. The packaged practice is Community-grade at best; Deci & Ryan's overjustification effect — already Established in `docs/planning-for-humans.md` §2 — is a specific warning that formalising a reward attaches strings to exactly the thing that worked *because* it had none; and entry 20 already refuses "anything algorithmic here" for the adjacent reason. The Menu's current, structural, unmeasured shape is already the safer version of what this practice is reaching for. - **V2-candidate**, narrow: let the category a person already sees be chosen at write time instead of silently defaulting to `read` — a two-tap choice at the same moment `heat.set` already asks one (ADR-0029's pattern), not a new surface, not a rank, not a requirement. This fixes a verified defect — a six-value schema field that is dead code in the shipped app — rather than building anything new. - **later**, and only as a question: whether the categories should read as cost or quickness ("something small," "something that takes a while") rather than activity type, since that is nearer what the practice is actually for and nearer what ADR-0065's capacity-aware offer could someday consult without a scored field. Not proposed as a build now — named so a future session does not reopen the question from nothing. - **refuse**, permanently: any completion or "used" event for a Menu item beyond the existing `menu.item.promoted`. Recording use is the one change that would let this entry's own risk back into the app.

### 27. The cost of recording decides whether recording happens

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — Every planner asks a person to pay something at the moment of writing a thing down: a tap, a field, a decision about where it goes. That price is charged at the exact moment the thing is most fragile — before it is anywhere — and it is charged against attention that is already spoken for. When the price rises, the recording does not get slower. It stops.

- **EVIDENCE** — **Strong.** Two pre-registered experiments (Gilbert group, *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology*; PMC11103908) manipulated the physical effort of setting a reminder against three levels of memory load: *"At all memory loads, reminder-setting was reduced when it was more effortful. The ability to set reminders allowed participants to compensate for the influence of memory load on accuracy in the low-effort condition; this effect was attenuated in the high-effort condition."* Two findings, not one — effortful recording is done less, **and the accuracy benefit it would have delivered is attenuated when it is done.** The second is the one that matters: the cost is not paid in convenience, it is paid in the thing the tool exists to provide. Read from the primary rather than a summary; the paradigm is Gilbert et al. (2020)'s optimal-reminders task.

- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — The industry standard is to charge at capture and call it structure: a project picker, a due date, a priority, a list to choose among, sometimes all four in one modal. Each is defensible alone and the sum is a toll gate in front of the one path that must stay clear. The tools that removed the toll removed it only from the first field — quick-capture into an inbox — and then rebuilt the whole gate one screen later, at sorting, where it does the same damage to the same person on the same day.

- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — More than most, and it arrived at it without this citation. Capture is one field, first on the screen, with focus on arrival; nothing is sorted, split or filed on the way in; the dump commit refuses a count; and sorting sits behind a door rather than in a corridor (ADR-0085), so a capture may change what the sorting surface shows and may never turn it on. The repo has stated the principle since before v1 — *deciding while dumping stops the dumping* — as an intuition with no experimental warrant. This is the warrant.

- **WHERE IT STILL BITES** — Filing, not capture. Attaching a place to a thing means opening its detail sheet, expanding, and scrolling, one item at a time. The sorting card offers six routes and a container route as first-class buttons and offers no way to say where a thing can be DONE. So the axis that the situated filters read (entry 24) is the axis whose recording costs the most, which is why a real store carries almost none of it — and a filter with nothing to filter on reads to its reader as a broken feature rather than an empty one.

- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **V2-candidate.** Put the context question on the sorting card beside the six routes and the container route, on the pattern the container route already uses: one tap from the card, a picker that makes one if it is not there, no new screen. Cost is a control on a deliberately spare surface and a budget raise; the finding above is the argument for paying it. **Refuse**, in the same breath: any requirement to answer it, any prompt that asks twice, any inference of a context from a title, and any treatment of an unlabelled item as incomplete — an unlabelled node already fits every answer by design (`fitsHere`), and that default is what stops the filter turning an ordinary store into an empty screen.

### 28. Trusting a store completely is what creates the exposure, not offloading itself

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — The promise this app makes is that it will hold things so a person does not have to. If that promise is kept perfectly, the person stops holding them. That is the intended effect and it is also the whole of the risk, and the two cannot be separated because they are the same mechanism.

- **EVIDENCE** — **Moderate**, from one well-powered recent experiment read in full, and a correction to a claim this catalogue nearly acquired. *"Let it go: How trusted reminders alter intention maintenance"* (N = 320; PMC13486017, 2026) crossed reminder reliability — 0%, 50%, 100% — with availability, using thought probes as a real-time measure of whether the intention stayed in awareness. First session: reminders improved performance **without** reducing intention-related thought, *"consistent with duplicative support"* — offloading added to internal memory rather than replacing it. Second session, after experiencing fully reliable support: those participants *"reduced PM-related thoughts, redistributed attention to the ongoing task, and showed reduced PM retrieval when reminders were unexpectedly withdrawn."* The 50% condition did not substitute at all — uncertainty kept people maintaining internally, and while performance fell on withdrawal it never fell below the no-reminder baseline. **The correction:** a large impairment effect for offloading is real in the list-learning and saving-effect literature and does **not** transfer to delayed intentions. The intention-offloading review (Gilbert, Boldt, Sachdeva, Scarampi & Tsai) reports the opposite here — *"Scarampi and Gilbert (2020, Experiment 1) also found that previous use of an offloading strategy did not influence subsequent unaided memory, at least in the short term"* — and frames the general worry against the Socratic complaint about tools without endorsing it. An earlier draft of this catalogue would have carried the impairment claim on the strength of a summary. It is recorded here as refused rather than quietly omitted, because a wrong citation is worse than a missing one and this file exists because of that failure mode.

- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Every planner that requires an account, syncs through a server it controls, or updates itself silently has arranged for its own reliability to be revocable by somebody other than its reader. The literature says that arrangement converts a benefit into an exposure at the moment it fails, and the failure is invisible until the day it matters.

- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — All of it, and for reasons stated as principle rather than as this. Data is never lost to updates: append-only log, additive-only migrations, a snapshot auto-exported before any migration (law 9). A new version waits rather than taking over, and the reader is told in words (Doctrine §7h) — a silently stale app **is** the unavailable store, exactly. There are no accounts and no server holding user data, so nobody can revoke it. And the coverage gauge is openable and self-exceptioning (ADR-0084), which is the piece this finding elevates most: if the exposure is created by trust, then a guarantee the reader can **check** is how trust gets calibrated instead of being asked for.

- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **SHIPPED**, and the entry exists to say why those four may not be traded away. The redistribution of attention to the task in front of you is what this product sells; the vulnerability is its mirror image, and there is no version that keeps one and refuses the other. One consequence is not yet met: **a way out that has never been exercised is not a mitigation.** The export exists and nothing establishes that a reader has ever successfully used it, which is the difference between a promise and a rehearsal. Treat that as the open half. **Refuse:** any account requirement, any server-held user data, any silent self-update, and any framing that presents reliability as a feature to be traded for convenience.

### 29. Miscalibration about one's own memory runs in both directions, so the app never adjudicates it

- **WHAT HAPPENS** — People decide whether to write something down by predicting whether they will otherwise forget it. That prediction is systematically wrong, the direction of the error differs between populations, and nothing on the outside can tell which error a given reader is making.

- **EVIDENCE** — **Strong** for the phenomenon and its replication; the population differences are well attested and their boundaries are not. Underconfidence in unaided memory is replicated across Boldt & Gilbert (2019), Engeler & Gilbert (2020), Gilbert (2015b), Gilbert et al. (2020) and Grinschgl et al. (2021), and it scales: *"the extent of underconfidence, or metacognitive bias, also increased as memory load increased"* — the fuller the pile, the more a person underestimates themselves. In the other direction, the intention-offloading review reports older adults *"were over-confident in their unaided ability to perform the task"* and *"did not fully compensate for impaired performance even when they were allowed to set reminders."* And the trigger is the prediction rather than the fact: people set reminders *"when they think that they will otherwise forget, not necessarily when they will actually forget."*

- **HOW CONVENTIONAL SYSTEMS MAKE IT WORSE** — Tools that comment on their own use — flagging that a reminder was unnecessary, summarising how many were set, offering to tidy the ones that were never needed — are adjudicating a prediction the person made about their own mind, using an outcome the person can no longer separate from having set the reminder in the first place. It is a verdict dressed as a tidy-up.

- **WHAT QUIETKEEP ALREADY DOES** — Nothing that touches it, which is the correct amount. Passing something over records nothing; the offer's refusal is event-free; there is no count of what was avoided; and no surface anywhere states how many reminders, clocks or captures a person has made.

- **ROUTING PROPOSAL** — **refuse**, twice and permanently. **First:** nothing may nudge a reader toward offloading less — no *you probably do not need a reminder for this*, no confidence prompt, no observation about volume. The bias runs opposite ways in different people, the app cannot tell which reader it has, and metacognitive training has been shown to correct the judgment without moving the behaviour, so the intervention would not work even where the diagnosis was right. **Second:** nothing may ever validate the prediction after the fact — no surface may imply a reminder turned out to be unnecessary. Both are the sort of well-meant addition a later session proposes on its own, which is why they are written down rather than left to taste. The load finding sharpens both: the guarantee matters most exactly when the pile is fullest, which is exactly when its reader is least able to believe they would have coped without it.
