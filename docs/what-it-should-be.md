# What it should be

Written **without opening the source**, from the demand alone. The comparison
against what exists comes last and deliberately last, because every design
document in this repo so far began by reading the code, finding a defect, and
correcting it — which makes the built thing the foundation when the built thing
is the experiment. Refinements of a wrong frame stay inside it.

---

## Part 1 — The demand

One person, one condition, stated without reference to any product.

**You are always thinking of something. You cannot trust it is the right
something, because you cannot see what you are not thinking of.**

The failure is not forgetting. It is that **coverage is unverifiable from the
inside**, and no amount of effort from inside can fix that — checking harder is
the same instrument reporting on itself.

Four things follow immediately, and they are the whole specification.

- **Capture does not solve it.** Writing one thing down relieves that one thing.
  It says nothing about the others, which is where the anxiety actually lives.
- **A list does not solve it.** A list shows what is *in* it. The question is
  what is *missing*, and no list can answer that about itself.
- **Only a claim about the container helps**, and it must be checkable cheaply,
  from outside, at any moment. If checking it is expensive, it will not be
  checked at the moment it is needed, which is the moment of doubt.
- **The trust is dichotomous.** A container that holds *most* things is not
  partly trusted. It is untrusted, and it does not recover — because one
  discovered exception retroactively invalidates every past assurance.

**It is therefore not a planner.** Planning is something you do once, in advance,
about a future you cannot predict. This is for **execution**: being shown the
right thing at the moment you can act. And it must be *shown*, not looked up,
because looking up requires knowing what to look for, which is the faculty that
is absent.

**The modal session is a return after an absence.** Not a daily ritual. Weeks
pass. Whatever the app asks of a person must be O(1) in the length of that
absence — the test being: *if somebody skips it for three weeks, does the data
degrade or does the app start lying?* If yes, it is homework, and homework is
what gets abandoned first.

## Part 2 — The design, derived

### There are exactly three moments

Everything a person does with this falls into three, and a fourth would be the
product inventing work for itself.

- **PUT.** Something arrived in my head. Get it out of my head. Cost as close to
  zero as physics allows.
- **NEXT.** I have some capacity right now. Tell me what to do with it.
- **PROOF.** Is the container holding? One look, no reading.

That is the app. Not a section list — the complete set.

### The screen

**One screen. One question, one proof, one way in.**

- A place to put something down, always present, never modal, never behind
  anything.
- One thing you could do now, named, with the reason it is being offered stated
  in one sentence from a small closed vocabulary.
- One line asserting the container holds, openable into how it knows.

Nothing else has a permanent place. Everything else is *maintenance of the
machine* — and maintenance must never sit between a thought and safety, or
between arriving and acting.

### The invariant, stated so it can be broken

Here is the promise, in the strongest form that is enforceable:

> **Everything you give it will be offered back to you as something you can act
> on, without you doing anything else to it.**

Note what that is *not*. It is not "everything has a clock." It is not "nothing
is silent." Those are properties of the schema. **The reader's question is
whether the thing comes back where they will meet it**, and a promise phrased in
schema terms can be true while the reader's version of it is false.

The clause that carries all the weight is **without you doing anything else to
it**. If an item must be categorised, routed, dated or filed before the app will
offer it, then the app does not hold it — it holds it *hostage*, and the ransom
is a decision made at the worst possible moment.

### What follows, and this is the load-bearing part

**An item is offerable the instant it exists.**

The title somebody typed is enough. *Buy milk* does not need a category, a
horizon, a heat, a place, or restating as a well-formed next action before the
app is allowed to hand it back. It is a thing. You can do it. Nothing further is
required and asking for anything further is the app charging for its own filing
system.

So the pipeline is not:

- capture → decide what it is → *now* eligible to be offered

It is:

- capture → **eligible to be offered** → optional refinement makes the offer
  smarter

That inversion is the design. It is what makes sorting genuinely optional for the
first time, because skipping it now costs **precision**, not **existence**. Under
the other order, "optional" is a word: skip it and the thing never comes back as
work, only as more sorting.

### So what is sorting FOR

Three things, and nothing else:

- **Anchor it** — to a time, or better, to an event or another item's
  completion, so it returns at the right moment instead of merely in turn.
- **Attach it** — under something, so that finishing it adds up to something.
- **Retire it** — say it was not a thing after all, or let it go without deleting
  it and without it counting as failure.

That is a much smaller job than a queue of forced choices, and none of it is
urgent, because none of it is load-bearing for the guarantee.

### Ordering, when the app knows almost nothing

Determinism is the accommodation. Same state in, same order out; nothing moves
without a human act; every offer states its reason.

The order, in full:

- Things anchored to a moment that has come.
- Things a person said they wanted sooner.
- Everything else in the order it arrived, stable.

An unrefined item sorts under the third rule, which is deterministic, needs no
inference, and is explainable in one sentence. **No learned rank, no
personalised score, no probabilistic ordering** — not a feature awaiting
evidence but a harm, because it destroys the detectability of one's own actions,
which is the only mechanism by which control over the system is learned.

### The objection, answered

*If unrefined items are offered, the offer becomes noise.* This is the real
objection and it is why one would be tempted to gate on sorting.

It does not survive contact:

- **The offer is one thing.** The cost of a poor offer is one decline. The cost
  of the alternative is that a whole day's captures are inert — held, but never
  shown as work, which is the guarantee failing in the way the reader will
  actually experience it.
- **Declining must be free and must record nothing.** Not a score, not a skip
  count, not a durable record of what somebody could not face. If declining is
  free, a wrong offer costs one tap and the system stays trusted.
- **The offer may say what it knows**, as a fact about the world: *put down on
  Tuesday, nothing else said about it.* Never a fact about the person.

### Relief has to be symmetric

Declining must end the demand as completely as doing it. If finishing quiets the
system and declining does not, then escape strictly dominates and the interface
has made the choice for the person. Whatever occupies the moment after an act is
what becomes attached to acting — so that moment belongs to settling, not to the
next offer sliding into the space.

### Capacity

A declaration, never a reading, never inferred, never asked for by a prompt. It
changes **which** things are offered and never **how many**. A good day buys a
harder thing, not a longer list. Only a person may raise their own ceiling.

And the app does not know which body it is in: the same declaration of a low day
means *offer less* for one condition and *offer anyway* for another, both
correct, each harmful to the other. So it is asked once, in the person's own
words, obeyed thereafter, and never revisited unprompted.

### What it says, and about whom

**Everything it says is a fact about the world. Nothing is a fact about the
person.** No inference from logs, no profile, no trend, no rate, no ratio, no
streak, no count of a window of past events. The person's own declaration is the
higher-validity instrument; what a tool infers from behaviour is precisely the
measure that does not track what matters.

**No counting anywhere the person arrives.** A number that only rises as things
are put down converts a good day into a visible debt, and a count of what is
outstanding is unfalsifiable to a reader — it says nothing about whether the
outstanding things are covered, which is the only question being asked.

### Warmth

Delivered structurally — by absorbing work and losing nothing — never verbally.
Verbal reassurance is a threat cue to the most self-critical reader, and an
approving opinion is still an opinion about a person. Not a coach, not a
companion, not an insight engine, not a tracker, not a dashboard.

### The shape never changes; the contents never repeat

The layout is fixed forever — that is the errorless-learning and familiarity
requirement, and it is why a control's position is a compatibility surface. The
contents are computed and different every time, which is why it is worth opening
when entirely familiar. It cannot rent attention from novelty.

## Part 3 — The comparison

Only now, and only as measurement.

### What the built thing gets right

- Capture is cheap, survives interruption, and never destroys text on failure.
- Append-only log, state as a fold, additive migrations. The data model is sound
  and is not the problem.
- The write boundary refuses to create something with no way back. The mechanism
  is right.
- Determinism, the closed reason vocabulary, no streaks, no "overdue", no
  inference about the person — all held, and gated.
- An exit that is neither done nor deleted exists.

### The gap, and it is one gap

**The guarantee the code enforces is not the guarantee the reader reads.**

The enforced invariant is *every node has a clock, a surface, a menu place, or a
clocked parent*. The read invariant is *it will come back to me as something I
can act on*. These come apart in exactly one place, and it is the widest path
into the app:

**A captured thought is covered but not offerable.** It is clocked at the moment
of writing — genuinely, in the same transaction — so it satisfies the enforced
invariant and appears in the proof as covered. But the offer surface excludes
anything not yet categorised. So it will come back **as more sorting**, never as
work.

For the reader that is the promise failing, and failing silently, with a green
badge over it. It is the same defect this repo already named once — *a clock
nobody reads is silence with paperwork* — one level up: **a covered item nobody
will ever be offered is coverage with paperwork.**

Everything else follows from that one inversion:

- The sorting queue exists because the offer is gated on it. Remove the gate and
  the queue has no reason to be a queue.
- The forced-choice card exists to drain the queue.
- The lighter preliminary pass exists to make the forced choice cheaper.
- The count exists to show the queue's depth.
- The corridor on arrival exists to get the queue drained.

**Five surfaces, and none of them is the product.** They are all consequences of
one decision: that an item must be classified before it may be offered.

**That decision was written down, with a reason, and the reason is where the
frame is visible.** ADR-0030 states it: an unrouted capture belongs to triage,
because offering it as work *asks the same question twice, in a surface whose
promise is that it has already decided*.

Take that seriously, because it is not a careless line — and it still does not
hold. It is true only if offering an unrouted item means **asking the routing
question about it**. It does not. Offering it means handing back the words
somebody typed, with *done* and *not now*. No question is asked twice, because
the routing question is not asked at all. And the surface's promise is that it
decided **what to show you** — not that every item has been classified. Handing
back *buy milk* in arrival order is the surface deciding.

So the premise smuggled in is that an unclassified item can only be *presented as
a classification decision*. That is the inherited assumption, and it was examined
and passed through rather than missed — which is worse than an oversight and much
more useful to know, because it means the frame survived scrutiny by being
invisible to it.

That is the answer to why building feature after feature closed no gap. Each was
a correct fix inside the frame, and the frame had a reason written under it.

### What today's two releases actually were

Honestly, because it matters more than the work:

- **1.42.0** made the guarantee openable and gave it reasons and exceptions. The
  surface is right and worth keeping. But it proves the *schema* invariant, so it
  will report full coverage over a store whose captures will never be offered.
  **It measures the weaker claim.** The fix is the invariant, not the surface.
- **1.42.1** removed the arrival corridor. Consistent with this design and
  survives it — but arrived at as a defect fix, and it treats a symptom. With the
  offer gate removed, the corridor has nothing to drain and would not exist to be
  removed.

Neither is wasted. Both were aimed one level below the thing that is wrong.

## Part 4 — What this makes the work

In dependency order, not priority order.

- **Make an unrefined item offerable.** One change of predicate at the offer
  boundary, plus an order rule for items the app knows nothing about, plus the
  offer stating what it knows without judging it. This is the whole design; the
  rest is consequence.
- **Restate the guarantee as the reader's version, and enforce that** — will be
  offered back as something to act on, not merely carries a clock. Then the
  proof's exceptions become real exceptions rather than schema artefacts.
- **Demote sorting to refinement.** Not a queue with a depth, not a pass to
  drain. A thing you may go and do, that changes when and where an item returns
  and never whether.
- **Then re-ask what the remaining surfaces are for**, each having lost its
  reason to exist as a stage. Some become inspection modes. Some go.

**What must be checked before any of it is built**, because this document was
written deliberately blind and blindness has a cost:

- ~~That the offer boundary excludes unclarified captures for the reason assumed
  here~~ — **checked, and this document was wrong.** It is recorded in ADR-0030
  with a reason, quoted and answered in Part 3. The design is unchanged; the
  claim that nobody wrote it down was false and is corrected.
- That nothing else silently depends on "unclarified" as a state.
- Whether a store with a large unrefined backlog produces a sensible offer under
  arrival order, on real data rather than a fixture.

Those are comparisons against the experiment. They are not the foundation, and
none of them can change Part 2.
